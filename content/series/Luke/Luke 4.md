# Jesus' Unwavering Devotion ([[Luke 4#1|Luke 4:1]][[Luke 4#2|]][[Luke 4#3|]][[Luke 4#4|]][[Luke 4#5|]][[Luke 4#6|]][[Luke 4#7|]][[Luke 4#8|]][[Luke 4#9|]][[Luke 4#10|]][[Luke 4#11|]][[Luke 4#12|-12]])
- "Full of the Holy Spirit" - Jesus is physically alone, but not spiritually. He is guided by the third member of the trinity.
- Benjamin L. Gladd observes that *testing*, 
>   contains two levels of meaning: (1) “to endeavor to discover the nature or character of something by testing” and (2) “to entice to improper behavior.” Given the Old Testament background to Jesus’s wilderness temptation in the Synoptics, one could argue that the devil is “testing” Jesus.
- "Tempted by the Devil" - While we have three accounts recorded. Jesus is very close to the end of his 40 days and Satan ramps up his attacks in three ways
	- "Hunger" - Jesus is hungry, his physical state is weakened opening him up for spiritual attack. Satan appeals to Jesus' human nature, "if you're hungry then you should eat" 
		- [[Luke 4#8|Luke 4:8]] - [[Deut 8#3|Deut 8:3]] [[Deut 6#13|Deut 6:13]]
	- "Authority" - Satan promises an easier way for Jesus to gain control of the earth, but it is a deception. By Jesus submitting to Satan he then would also relinquish Heaven as well. (This observation from Benjamin L. Gladd in *From the Manger to the Throne*)
		- [[Luke 4#10|Luke 4:10]] - [[Ps 91#11|Ps 91:11]][[Ps 91#12|-12]]
	- "Unbelief" - Satan tempts Jesus to *not* trust God, but to seek for a sign. If Jesus was to jump and be caught by angels, here he has tested God (in an improper way) and found that he is valued. Instead Jesus exercises restraint, trusting in the Holy Spirit within him, the baptism he experienced, and the words of scripture.  
> 		- "Jesus, as the son of Adam, as the new Israel, and as the King of Israel, is called upon to put his trust in the Lord, and he must not put God to the test, demanding the Lord to show his faithfulness in an extraordinary way." -Thomas R. Schreiner
		- [[Luke 4#12|Luke 4:12]] - [[Deut 6#16|Deut 6:16]]
- "Opportune time" - We shall see Satan and his temptation again ("Get away from me Satan!" [[Mark 8#33|Mark 8:33]])

# Jesus' Authority over Man ([[Luke 4#14|Luke 4:14]][[Luke 4#15|]][[Luke 4#16|]][[Luke 4#17|]][[Luke 4#18|]][[Luke 4#19|]][[Luke 4#20|]][[Luke 4#21|]][[Luke 4#22|]][[Luke 4#23|]][[Luke 4#24|]][[Luke 4#25|]][[Luke 4#26|]][[Luke 4#27|]][[Luke 4#28|]][[Luke 4#29|]][[Luke 4#30|-30]])
- "Returned in the Power of the Spirit" - Jesus is fatigued from his fast in the desert, but he is spiritually strong
- "Taught in synagogues" - he's teaching in the power of the spirit and people are astounded
- Our first look at the **authority** of Jesus' teaching as an adult
- He is glorified. 

> [!QUESTION]
  **What is different about Jesus' time in Galilee and Nazareth?** 
  We see that Jesus is *not* glorified this time around. The people *marvel* but they do not believe. They are entertained. We see this in the question of how they say, "Hey, that's Joseph's lil' boy!"

  - "stood up to read" - common in how people read God's word
- [[Luke 4#18|Luke 4:18]][[Luke 4#19|-19]] - [[Isa 61#1|Isa 61:1]][[Isa 61#2|-2]]
- "marveled at his gracious words" - We see they respond like the people in Galilee at first
- "What you did at Capernaum" - Galilee is the region, the city is Capernaum. When he returned in the spirit to Galilee ([[Luke 4#14|Luke 4:14]]) he must have came through Capernaum
- "Is this not Joseph's boy" - We can observe things go well at first, but sour with this phrase! Thomas Schreiner observes, "Even those in small towns can become snobbish and elitist and jealous when one from their own circle prospers." Also, is this ignorance or willful unbelief in Jesus' immaculate conception.
- Jesus shuts down the listeners by observing that they are attending *simply* to marvel. He is a spectacle, simply here to do the party trick of healing, like he did in Capernum. Jesus, explains that not the case, and furthermore that those listening would get passed over from salvation. 
	- "Both the widow of Zarephath ([[1 Kgs 17#8|1 Kgs 17:8]][[1 Kgs 17#9|]][[1 Kgs 17#10|]][[1 Kgs 17#11|]][[1 Kgs 17#12|]][[1 Kgs 17#13|]][[1 Kgs 17#14|]][[1 Kgs 17#15|]][[1 Kgs 17#16|]][[1 Kgs 17#17|]][[1 Kgs 17#18|]][[1 Kgs 17#19|]][[1 Kgs 17#20|]][[1 Kgs 17#21|]][[1 Kgs 17#22|]][[1 Kgs 17#23|]][[1 Kgs 17#24|-24]]) and Naaman the Syrian ([[2 Kgs 5#1|2 Kgs 5:1]]) were Gentiles... God bypassed all the widows and lepers in Israel, yet showed grace to the Gentiles." - J. Mac.
> [!QUESTION]
  **Why are the crowds angry?** 1. They are not "chosen" just like the widows in Elijah's time 2. They will not be cleansed like the lepers in Elisha's time 3. J. Mac explains, "what seems to have sparked the Nazarenes' fury was Christ's suggestion that divine grace might be withheld form them, yet extended to the Gentiles"

- Our first look at the **divinity** of Jesus' **power** as he passes through the people mysteriously

"went to desolate place" - Most likely to pray
"I **must** go preach" - Jesus *will* follow God's commands relentlessly. Despite the clamor and desire for him to stay he will first and foremost follow's God's guidance.

# Ties the Episodes
Power and Authority: Satan promises false authority to Jesus. Authority that would in turn subvert Jesus' forthcoming authority. Jesus denies this fake authority and we see His true God-ordained authority appear when he willingly submits to the plans of the Father and the Spirit. Furthermore we see the power and authority of God's word in:
- The Old Testament passages that Jesus quotes
- The teaching in the synagogues
- The healing words of Jesus to humanity
- The holy rebuke of Jesus to demons

# Application
This week's application is applicable in both our marriage and in our personal lives and it is for us to fight temptation like how Jesus fought temptation. How do we see Jesus fight?
- **Jesus recalls scripture**
- **Jesus values God over his physical state.** He *is* hungry, but he values God the Father more. He knows, in this situation, it would go against the plans of God to create food. The Spirit has lead him to this wilderness to undergo this trial. For him to break his fast early, is not what God wills. So he focuses on pleasing the Father more. 
  *[[Bible/ESV/Deuteronomy/Deut 8#3|Deuteronomy 8:3]] And he humbled you and let you hunger and fed you with manna, which you did not know, nor did your fathers know, that he might make you know that man does not live by bread alone, **but man lives by every word that comes from the mouth of the LORD.***  
- **Jesus values God over his standing/authority**. Certainly an ironic one given the rest of the chapter but we see Satan tempt Jesus with having authority sooner. Authority without suffering on the cross. However, Jesus again values God over anything Satan can promise. 
  *[[Bible/ESV/Deuteronomy/Deut 6#13|Deuteronomy 6:13]] It is the LORD your God you shall fear. Him you shall serve and by his name you shall swear.*  
- **Jesus trusts God**. This seems so obvious, but goodness aren't their times in intense temptation where you don't? This particular temptation is so much like the serpent in the garden, "Didn't God say he would have angels minister to you?" Yes he did! But that does not mean Jesus needed to put God to the test! God's word was enough, including the one that told Jesus how to act. 
  *[[Bible/ESV/Deuteronomy/Deut 6#16|Deuteronomy 6:16]] "You shall not put the LORD your God to the test, as you tested him at Massah.*  

So for us to consider:
- When tempted do you bring scripture to mind to overcome your worldly feelings (hunger, pain, despair, frustration, etc.)?
- When tempted do you bring scripture to mind to overcome your worldly desires (leadership, wealth, etc.)?
- When tempted do you bring scripture to mind to remember to trust God?
- How can you, as a couple, help each other to fight temptation?

> "Let us note... our Lord Jesus Christ’s ability to sympathize with those who are tempted. This is a truth that stands out prominently in this passage. Jesus has been really and literally tempted himself. It was fitting that he who came to “destroy the devil’s work” (1 John 3:8) should begin his own work with a special conflict with Satan. Above all, it was fitting that the great High Priest and advocate of sinners should be one who has had personal experience of conflict and has known what it is to be in the fire. “He himself suffered when he was tempted” (Hebrews 2:18). How much he suffered we cannot tell. But that his pure and spotless nature did suffer intensely, we may be sure. Let all true Christians take comfort in the thought that they have a friend in heaven who can be “touched with the feeling of [their] infirmities” (Hebrews 4:15)." - J. C. Ryle
