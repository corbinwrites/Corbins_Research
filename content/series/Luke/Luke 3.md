# Episodes
1. John the Baptist's Ministry (Luke 3:1-20)
2. Jesus' Baptism (Luke 3:21-22)
3. Jesus' Adoptive Genealogy (Luke 3:23-38)

## John the Baptist's Ministry (Luke 3:1-20)

J.C. Ryle notes John is quite fierce in his declaration of Hell, "the coming wrath" and "the ax." He does not shrink back from boldly declaring the truth. 

Schreiner states, "The nation is at the same place it was when it wandered in the wilderness for forty years and when it was in exile for seventy years in Babylon... The nation must go through the Jordan again, so to speak, and thus they are baptized, that is, immersed, in the Jordan to symbolize their cleansing from sin."

"Every valley shall be filled" - Certainly, at first blush, feels like war-like imagery. Where we get this idea of the Messiah being a mighty conquerer, the Messiah will level mountains and valleys. But we know that Jesus has arrived to make war on our hearts. This is what both John and Jesus are working to level. 

John says it's not about your heritage! It's about being repentant. 
- "We have Abraham as our father"
- "God can raise up children from Abraham from the rocks"
- "The ax is laid to the root of the trees"


John provides an interesting framework in presenting a bible message:
- Bold declaration of the truth of God
- Practical, loving application so people know how to apply it

v. 16 "baptize you with Holy Spirit and fire." - Ryle states it could be either a prophecy of Pentecost or simply a note to the refining fire of the Spirit.

### What happens?
John begins his ministry and baptizing and instructing people for the repentance of sins

## Jesus' Baptism (Luke 3:21-22)

### What happens?
Jesus is baptized and the Triune God verifies his standing as the Messiah and as God the Father's Son.

## Jesus' Adoptive Genealogy (Luke 3:23-38)

The prevailing thought is that Matthew follows the royal line (who rights are given to whom) while Luke follows the biological line (who physically gave life to whom). Luke would follow the Levirate line. 

### What happens?
Luke details how Jesus has been adopted into the line of David


# What ties them together?
I'm thinking it could be a look at the deity of Christ. At Jesus' baptism God calls him "My Son", but we also see that Adam is the Son of Man. We have this idea of the "Better Adam" coming to light here. With the combination of the two. 

I think Luke here finishes setting the stage for Christ. The herald has just stopped speaking, we have read his credentials. We are stirred to great anticipation and next we will meet him face-to-face in the first part of his ministry.

# Application
John's bold proclamation to repent from sin shows us to live in a similar way with those around us and especially our spouse. Certainly, an exhortation is needed from time to time. Notice *what* John is doing here, he brings a Word from God to the people of Israel. We can similarly do that for our spouse, but our word will come from the Word (the Bible). John has watched Israel walk further and further into sin, he has to wake them up. Sometimes we need to do that for our spouse.

An example I can think about in recent history was when Rachelle called me out on my pride. She noted that I had, at the time, frequently felt like I was the subject of great injustice all the time. She asked, "Are you being prideful? Are you saying that your way is the right way for everyone to do everything?" What a great question. The answer was yes! I was attempting to supplant God as ruler!. Rachelle's exhortation stung, but it allowed me to grow as a Christian. Truly I was the "brood of vipers" in that moment. 

But John the Baptist (and Rachelle Harris) don't stop there. After exhortation is a loving application. People knew they needed to repent and asked John, what do I do? John does not respond with anger, but practically gives steps that meet that person with where they are in life. Different practices for the tax collector and soldier that still point them to repentance. Don't miss this crucial step in exhortation! If the person who is listening to your exhortation sees the need for repentance, sit with them and offer to guide with application. 

I would encourage you, when practicing exhortation to reflect on:

Hebrews 3:13 But exhort one another every day, as long as it is called "today," that none of you may be hardened by the deceitfulness of sin.  
*We are commanded to do this*

2 Timothy 4:2 preach the word; be ready in season and out of season; reprove, rebuke, and exhort, with complete patience and teaching.  
*We should do so with patience and a desire to teach (think on how John gives application)*

Proverbs 15:1 A soft answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger.  
*We should do so with kindness and love*
