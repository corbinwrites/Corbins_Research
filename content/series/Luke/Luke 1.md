# Intro
Luke writes to *Theophilus*, which means *Lover of God*. Some scholars speculate that this was a "stand-in" name for the reader. But more realistically, this was truly this man's name and God orchestrated things beautifully that he was the man who funded this book. We join the literal, "Lover of God" as fellow "lovers of God."

Note how much Luke mentions *accuracy* here:
- Luke "followed all things closely" ([[Luke 1#3|Luke 1:3]])
- In order to "write an orderly account" as opposed to a disorderly account. ([[Luke 1#3|Luke 1:3]])
- He has written in a way that Theophilus would "have certainty concerning the things you have been taught." ([[Luke 1#4|Luke 1:4]])
## What happens in this Episode?
Luke assures Theophilus that he will have certainty in what is taught

## Personal Takeaway
We can trust everything here is true! Luke writes that this book will increase Theophilus' certainty about Jesus. We can rest assured that it will increase *our* certainty about Jesus too.

# Zechariah's humbling ([[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#5|Luke 1:5-24]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#6|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#7|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#8|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#9|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#10|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#11|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#12|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#13|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#14|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#15|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#16|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#17|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#18|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#19|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#20|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#21|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#22|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#23|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#24|]])
## Who *(Names)*
God answers the prayers of a priest named Zechariah, *God Remembers* and his wife Elizabeth, *God is My Promise*. They have always wanted a child and God gives them one named John, *God is Gracious*.

Theme - Great Reversal: A priest with the special task to burn incense. A high and mighty honor, humbled when he meets Gabriel and questions the angel, "how should I know this?"

## What happens in this Episode?
God answers the prayers of a priest and his wife and grants them a song who will proclaim the coming Messiah. Zechariah does not believe Gabriel and is afflicted with muteness for his sin. 

## What do we see about God?
We see God's graciousness extend, but we also see his justice and power balanced as he afflicts Zechariah for a few months with muteness for not taking seriously God's Promise! J.C. Ryle writes, “the punishment fit the offense. The tongue was not ready to speak the language of praise so it was struck [silent]” 



**Why does Elizabeth hide herself away?** No idea. J.C. Ryle and John Piper breeze past it. J. Mac tackles it head on and says she most likely was hiding herself away as a sign of appreciation. Thomas Schreiner offers probably the more helpful perspective by agreeing with John MacArthur and then stating, "we can't know for sure!" What's important here? That Elizabeth is *so* appreciative to God! She delights in the good gift God gave her, unlike her husband!

## Personal Takeaway
God, outside of time, knows that this righteous man would sin, yet still chooses him to father John the Baptist. He hears the prayers of this couple and blesses them with a child. He sees how an older woman is "reproached" among her people. God displays his graciousness to this man and woman with a good gift, just as he extends his graciousness to us daily.

# Mary's exaltation ([[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#26|Luke 1:26-38]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#27|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#28|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#29|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#30|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#31|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#32|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#33|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#34|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#35|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#36|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#37|]][[Bible/ESV/Luke/Luke 1#38|]])
Theme - Great Reversal: A single, unmarried woman named Mary, *bitterness and rebellion* from Nazareth, *stick town* will now bear the savior of the world. Certainly different from Zechariah and Elizabeth!

## What happens in this episode?
Mary meets Gabriel who tells her that she will bear the Messiah. Mary humbly submits to the will of God.

Notice how Gabriel adds that with God nothing is impossible. An encouragement to have faith! God can do whatever will bring him glory! A huge contrast from Zecheriah.

## What happens in this episode?
Mary goes to see Elizabeth and John leaps with delight! Elizabeth is filled with the Holy Spirit and prophecies blessing and that Jesus *is* the Messiah. 

Note: that this is the first time that person acknowledges Jesus as master, king, lord of all. She sees this through the Spirit!

## John the Baptist is born ([[Luke 1#57|Luke 1:57]][[Luke 1#58|]][[Luke 1#59|]][[Luke 1#60|]][[Luke 1#61|]][[Luke 1#62|]][[Luke 1#63|]][[Luke 1#64|]][[Luke 1#65|]][[Luke 1#66|-66]])
## What happens in this episode?
Elizabeth gives birth to John. The family around Zechariah and Elizabeth want to name the child Zechariah, but both Elizabeth and Zechariah stop them. After Zechariah declares that the child's name is John, Zechariah can speak again and he immediately worships God.

J.C. Ryle observes, “Zecheriah now believes every word that Gabriel told him and obeys every word of that message.” Ryle goes on to celebrate how affliction can bring us closer to God. Note how the first thing out of his mouth, after John, is praise!

Thomas Schreiner observes that Zecheriah declares the child’s name IS John. It has been decided already. This is a show of obedience!

## Zechariah worships God ([[Luke 1#67|Luke 1:67]][[Luke 1#68|]][[Luke 1#69|]][[Luke 1#70|]][[Luke 1#71|]][[Luke 1#72|]][[Luke 1#73|]][[Luke 1#74|]][[Luke 1#75|]][[Luke 1#76|]][[Luke 1#77|]][[Luke 1#78|]][[Luke 1#79|]][[Luke 1#80|-80]])
## What happens in this episode?
Zechariah prophesies that God has provided redemption, salvation, through the coming Messiah. He prophesies that John will be called a prophet and give knowledge and forgiveness due to the mercy of God. 

# Why these episodes?
Obviously these are the beginning of the Messiah being born. But Luke shows us that even the prophet coming before Jesus has a special place in the divine story. Both the forerunner and the Messiah are gracious gifts from a good Father to his children. Both the families who would raise them and the world.

# Major Themes
## God's Grace
- Still answers Zechariah's prayers knowing he would sin
- Allows a humble girl to bear the savior of the World
- Grants salvation, redemption, to the world to wholly undeserving
## Mary's Humble Devotion to God
## Triune God Present
- God the Father communicating his will through Gabriel
- Jesus in the Womb
- Two appearances of the Holy Spirit

# Application
Grace, while perfected by God, is a *communicable* attribute. We can show grace to those around us. We can be kind, grant gifts, help someone with their desires despite them being wholly undeserving. This is especially true in our marriages. In a far lesser way to God showing grace to Zechariah, could we not show grace to our spouse when he or she is in a bad mood, hungry, or tired? To be able to grant him/her a good gift that, frankly, their attitude has indicated that he/she is undeserving of. 

What are some ideas of ways you can show grace to your spouse?
