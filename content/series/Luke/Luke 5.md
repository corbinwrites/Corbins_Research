# Questions 
What episodes?
What do we see in this chapter about Jesus and his character?
Why does Luke put these episodes together?

# Episodes
1. Jesus shows authority over nature
2. Jesus shows authority over cleanliness
3. Jesus shows authority over sin
4. Jesus shows who he has come to save

# Jesus shows authority over nature/Belief of the first 3 disciples
- Peter is tired, but believes. He does lay down the net. We see he is rewarded for it. 
- See how Peter is humbled immediately in verse 8. He sees his own insufficiency at the generosity of Jesus and speaks to him with respect. 
	- Schreiner observes that, *"Jesus demonstrates to Peter that he is Lord over all of life, not only religious areas. He knows far more about fishing than Peter does."*
- Jesus instead brings comfort and gives Peter his new task. 
- We see a lot of belief in Simon, James, and John. They leave their profession, their wares to sell, their family, even the fish to follow Jesus. We see how these three men value God over everything.
# Jesus shows authority over cleanliness/Belief of the leper
- The man with leprosy believes in Jesus. He simply states that Jesus can perform this miracle. The leper knows Jesus to be the Messiah and that he can perform this task. 
- "*If one touched an unclean person, one became unclean, especially if the person had a skin disease. Jesus shows his compassion and love ([[Mark 1#41|Mark 1:41]]) by touching him—and a remarkable thing happens. Jesus stretches out his hand to save, just as Yahweh stretches out his hand to save. **The cleanness of Jesus is so powerful that it purifies those who are defiled;** it as if his power radiates to the one he touches, transforming him. Another remarkable feature of the story is the will of Jesus. It is his desire to touch and transform the one who is unclean, showing his compassion and love.*" - Thomas Schreiner
- Jesus rewards this faith
- Interesting how Jesus asks the man to follow the old testament law
> [!QUESTION]
> Why does Jesus tell the (former) leper to go to the temple? 
> *"He knew well that the last days of the Levitical institutions were close at hand and that they were soon to be laid aside forever. But so long as they were not abrogated, he would have them respected." - J.C. Ryle*
- Notice how Jesus retreats to be with God the Father

# Jesus shows authority over sin/Belief of the paralytic
- Interesting that the Pharisees and teachers had come to hear Jesus. Apparently he was quickly causing a stir. 
- First time we see Jesus forgive sin!
	- Jesus even proves this further by healing the paralytic so we may have certainty
- Again we see glorifying God (very good!)
- Thomas Schreiner shares this is the first occurence of "Son of Man" we see in Luke ([[Luke 5#24|Luke 5:24]]) which is in reference to [[Dan 7#13|Dan 7:13]][[Dan 7#14|-14]]

# Jesus shows who he has come to save/Belief of the tax collector(s)
- Jesus talks to Matthew. Here Luke uses Matthew's Hebrew name, perhaps with intention. By seeing Matthew is Jewish we can know that he is a "defector" in the eyes of Jewish society. Tax Collectors worked for Rome. This guy was serving the enemy.
	- This is who Jesus calls to be his disciple
- Matthew celebrates this and shows hospitality to Jesus and his friends. 
- Jesus explains this is who he has come for, those who are sick
	- Great Reversal: Those who are sick will be made well. Those who are sinners will be brought to repentance
- Fasting - "The fact that these Pharisees raised this question shows that they thought of fasting as a public exercise to display one's own spirituality." - J. Mac
	- *"The Pharisees agree with Jesus about the need for repentance, but they question whether Jesus understands what repentance is. The Baptist seemed to grasp repentance, for his disciples regularly fasted and prayed... In the Baptist and his disciples the Pharisees recognize godliness, but Jesus’ disciples live an entirely different life. They are eating, drinking, rejoicing, and feasting. Such joy and conviviality do not accord with traditional conceptions of righteousness... The religious life is marked by self-denial and sacrifice."* -  Thomas Schreiner
- We see "bridegroom" again! We had seen this with John and it has come back again!

> [!Question]
> **What does Jesus' parable mean?** You cannot mix the old with the new. There was an original way to do things, but there is a day forthcoming. He's contrasting the "old fasting" with the "new fasting." The old fasting was for the Messiah to come. The new fasting is for the messiah who has left. 

- Interesting how Jesus contrasts this sharply with his command for the man to give proper notice and sacrifice to the temple for the leprosy. 
- Both J. Mac & Schreiner observe that [[Luke 5#39|Luke 5:39]] is Jesus likely observing how the Pharisees are already and will continue to act. They are satisfied with the old way of living and the rituals that come with it. 
- "New Wine" Schreiner observes that this is an indication of vibrant new life! 
  *"The new message of Jesus cannot be contained or summed up by the spiritual practices of the Baptist and the Pharisees. **Putting new wine into old wineskins will burst the wineskins and ruin them, and the new wine will be wasted as well.** There is a joy, a newness, a wedding-like celebration that has arrived with the coming of Jesus as the bridegroom. Fresh and new wine must be put into new wineskins. The teaching and ministry of Jesus burst through the categories of spirituality promulgated by the Pharisees and transcend the Baptist as well, for the time of fulfillment has come."* -Thomas Schreiner


# Thread
Authority: We see Jesus command, nature, cleanliness, and sin 
Belief: We see people come to Jesus with great belief and faith

# Application
We begin to see Jesus' compassion pour out here in this chapter we see:
- Teach to a crowd of people
- Provide fish for the fisherman
- Invite Simon, James and John to join him as disciples (implication forgiving their sins)
- Ignores uncleanliness and cleans a leper with touch
- Healed great crowds
- Forgave the sins of and healed the paralytic ()
- Invited Matthew to join him as a disciple
- At dinner with the social pariahs 

In this chapter, specifically, notice how Jesus' compassion over comes social pressures. Jesus loves the unlovely. To the point where he touches an unclean man and share friendship with traitors to Israel. 

In our marriage, and in our lives, could we strive to show this kind of compassion? This kind of love. Jesus overcome social pressure to show his compassion. Can you overcome factors that hinder your compassion? Perhaps, like Jesus, it's being compassionate to someone who is not well-liked. Perhaps, in your marriage, it's showing compassion despite your spouse being angry, tired, hungry, or something else. How can you persist in showing compassion?
