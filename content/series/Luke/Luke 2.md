# Birth of Jesus ([[Luke 2#1|Luke 2:1]][[Luke 2#2|]][[Luke 2#3|]][[Luke 2#4|]][[Luke 2#5|]][[Luke 2#6|]][[Luke 2#7|-7]])
- God orchestrates the Roman census to fulfill the prophecy that Jesus is born in Bethlehem, the city of David, yet he is raised in Nazareth, stick town. Thus Jesus is the root of Jesse very directly. 
- Very clearly we see the humility of Jesus, where he is born in a simple feeding trough. God, in three persons, chooses this location for Jesus to be born. Already, we see great humility in Jesus in his birth. 
### What happens?
Mary & Joseph, due to the census, travel from Nazareth to Bethlehem, where Mary gives birth to Jesus.

So hear we see a single event that pierces through the dark veil of worldly existence and shows just a taste of the greatness, beauty, and perfection of God and of the Heavenly realm.

- The shepherds go over immediately to see the baby Jesus and to worship and glorify God. What a joy that Mary and Joseph are surrounded with people to celebrate this new baby. We see that Mary, "treasures all these things in her heart." A gracious gift from the Lord for two travelers, to be surrounded by community. 

### What happens?
Shepherds in the fields of Bethlehem are the first to hear and praise the coming of the Messiah from the Heavenly Host

# The Priest and Prophetess Give Glory to God ([[Luke 2#22|Luke 2:22]][[Luke 2#23|]][[Luke 2#24|]][[Luke 2#25|]][[Luke 2#26|]][[Luke 2#27|]][[Luke 2#28|]][[Luke 2#29|]][[Luke 2#30|]][[Luke 2#31|]][[Luke 2#32|]][[Luke 2#33|]][[Luke 2#34|]][[Luke 2#35|]][[Luke 2#36|]][[Luke 2#37|]][[Luke 2#38|]][[Luke 2#39|]][[Luke 2#40|-40]])

- We see that Mary and Joseph are faithful to God. They follow the law of the Lord and set out to offer sacrifice to God. 
	- "*What was it like to be the parents of Jesus? Luke reminds us that Joseph and Mary were marked by ordinary, everyday obedience to what God commanded. They experienced some remarkable events at Jesus’ birth, including the words of Simeon and Anna recorded here. But most of their lives consisted of trusting and obeying the Lord day in and day out, year in and year out. The Lord wants us to give ourselves to him even if the days we live in are not particularly exciting."* - Thomas Schreiner on Luke
- We encounter *another priest* who has heard from the Lord. 
- It's clear why Luke includes this prophecy, while the Angels declare the reign of Jesus is world wide, Simeon is the first to specifically mention the Gentiles!
- Simeon makes it clear that Jesus will be a dividing line. People will rise and fall by his name and teaching. 
- We also see one of the first evangelists (joining the shepherds in this task) as Anna goes forth and shares the Messiah has arrived with everyone who has been waiting.

### What happens?
Mary and Joseph take Jesus to the temple for purification where Simeon, the priest, is inspired by the Spirit to bless and prophecy. He is joined with Anna who does similarly. 

# Jesus Glorifies God ([[Luke 2#41|Luke 2:41]][[Luke 2#42|]][[Luke 2#43|]][[Luke 2#44|]][[Luke 2#45|]][[Luke 2#46|]][[Luke 2#47|]][[Luke 2#48|]][[Luke 2#49|]][[Luke 2#50|]][[Luke 2#51|]][[Luke 2#52|-52]])
We see Jesus' humility and deference to God the Father extend from physical surroundings (his birth), into his characteristics and mentality. He spends time listening and learning for three days about God the Father.
- Jesus has entered Jerusalem for Passover. A special feast where the people of Israel remember how God provided for them during the Exodus. 
- The is the third time, in Luke's account, that Jesus has been here (Circumcision, Purification, Passover) though it's safe to assume Jesus had been far frequently. Schreiner remarks that since Jesus is on the cusp of adulthood, it's possible this is his first time attending Passover. Remember this is historical biography so we're hitting highlights! This particular occasion was a bit more meaningful.
- We see that Jesus is asking question despite having the answers. His answer to Mary is true, he truly does want to be in the Father's house. 
- Schreiner observes that Jesus relationship changes with his parents as he gets older. Note the in his statement, "I *must* be my Father's House." Jesus' ultimate purpose is to please God and fulfill God's plan.

### What Happens?
Jesus stays in the temple when his parents leave and spends the entire time learning from the rabbis and asking questions and sharing answers. He shares with his parents that he desires to be with his Father God. 

# Themes
## Great Reversal
- Jesus, King of the Universe, born in a manger
- Unclean Shepherds, are shown the heavenly host and the first to hear of Jesus' birth
- Prophecy of Simeon, where Jesus will exalt the lowly and strike down the high and mighty
## Gospel Expansion
- Chapter 1 - Zechariah prophesies about how Jesus is deliverance for Israel
- Chapter 2 - Simeon prophesies about how Jesus is deliverance for *Gentiles*
	- The Shepherds leave the manger rejoicing and celebrating and "those who heard wondered"
	- Anna speaks to "all who were waiting for the redemption"

# Why these stories together?
Rachelle said, we have certainty that Jesus is the messiah! The angels, shepherds, priest and prophetess declare this fact. The location, events, and words spoken are the fulfillment of prophecy and are a testament to this fact. We see that Jesus himself knows it in the temple!
### Prophetic Passages Fulfilled!
[[Mic 4#8|Mic 4:8]],[[Mic 5#2|Mic 5:2]], [[2 Sam 7#12|2 Sam 7:12]][[2 Sam 7#13|-13]], [[Isa 9#6|Isa 9:6]][[Isa 9#7|-7]], [[Isa 42#6|Isa 42:6]], [[Isa 49#6|Isa 49:6]], [[Mal 3#1|Mal 3:1]]

We see the humility of Jesus contrasted by the triune God seeking to glorify him. God the Father commands the heavenly host to enter Earth and sing songs of praise. The Spirit comes upon Simeon and Anna and proclaims great praise and prophecy for him. Conversely we see Jesus seeking to glorify God in *his* actions by spending extended time in the temple. We see that this, in turn, leads to Jesus growing in stature and favor with God. 

# Application
Jesus exhibits great humility and devotion in this chapter. Humility is seen both in his physical birth and later in his spiritual development as we see when he sits under the teaching of the rabbis and submits to his earthly parents as well. This is despite the fact that Jesus, being God, has all power and authority granted to him through God, yet he does not exercise it. He submits under earthly authority as well as the authority of God the Father. This is true humility
Though not the *only* contributing factor, Jesus' humility and devotion leads Jesus to be "increased in wisdom and in stature and in favor with *God and man.*" [[Luke 2#52|Luke 2:52]]

I like that this section does not only say that Jesus was favored by God, but also people. *People liked Jesus!* Sometimes I see people in the church who pursue a godly characteristic in an abrasive way. Sometimes I'm one of those people! Often times in my marriage I have been known to "defer the decision" to Rachelle as a false sense of humility. "Oh I'm being humble to the Lord by letting her choose dinner." But then I'm bitter when she chooses Mendocino Farms over a Taqueria. I'm sure Rachelle won't like my much if I'm moping over my salad, that's not a humility that gains favor with people *or* with God. 

Instead I need a true humility that looks like Jesus here. The Word of Life, creator of the universe, sitting down at the feet of a Jewish teacher to learn about what he could possibly already know. If he *did not* know and was genuinely learning it, then still look to the humility in submitting to his parents and tearing himself away from the place he most desired to be to serve Mary and Joseph. He does so with perfect submission.
