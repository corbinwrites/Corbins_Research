## Preamble

There are a few things that are a little funky on the chronology of events presented. Again, it’s important to note that this is a *song,*and that it’s being sung for a purpose. There’s a reason the Psalmist presents things in this order and why he mentions the events that he does. That reason is to illustrate the main core meaning of this Psalm, **God is faithful even when his people are not.**

> [!question] ❓
> What does it mean to be faithful?
> _Remaining loyal and steadfast_

## Praise (v. 1-3)

A fascinating quality that is present in both last week’s Psalm and in this one, is an anchor statement for us. The Psalmist, up front, tells what this is all about, the Lord’s “steadfast love.” I know we have encountered this word many times before but I wanted to pull out this distinct form of “love.” This love is very particular to God, in other translations it may be rendered as “loving-kindness” or “covenant love.” The main idea in all translations is that the love is persistent, long-suffering. Here the Psalmist says the length of time that the Lord’s love endures for… forever! There’s is no end, no limit, to his love for his people. In many ways, we see what we think could be some limits to that in this passage, yet His love over comes. **God is faithful forever.**

John MacArthur quickly points out that the answer to the question in verse 2 is verse 3. Those who are just and righteous, can declare the mighty deeds of the Lord.

Please do keep in mind that this question, like others that we have encountered in the Psalm is actually rhetorical. Though if we were to tease MacArthur’s idea a bit further the image could be quite contemporary. It presents the image of a modern day “call to worship” for the psalm. The Psalmist strums harp a bit while the “keyboard” swells. The Psalmist asks the congregation, “Who can utter the mighty deed of the Lord and declare his praise?” The congregation responds, “those who observe justice and do righteousness at all times!”

The Psalmist gives us a reminder of _who_ is singing this Psalm. The people of the Lord that act in this way. They are a people who *should *observe justice and who do righteousness at all times.

> [!question] ❓
> What does it mean to observe justice?
> _Act in a way that is just, upright, correct way of the Lord_

What does it mean to do righteousness at all times?
_Similar! This uses synonymous parallelism_

The people of God, remind each other of this standard

## Petition (v. 4-5)

We start our song with a personal reflection! As many of our worship songs today do, the song shifts to the individual singing in the congregation (I Am Not My Own by the Gettys, Give Me Jesus by Fernando Ortega)

The Psalmist intends that the Israelite congregation all individually petition the Lord to count them as a part of the people of God.

Important to note: The Psalmist *is *already a part of God’s chosen and he would like to maintain that status. The Psalmist, foreshadowing the confession to come, knows how the Lord has saved Israel in the past and has confidence he will do so in the future. He asks the Lord to be faithful to him, just as the Lord has been faithful to Israel in the past, so that he may also prosper, rejoice, and glory with the people of God.

## Confession: God’s Faithfulness and Israel’s Unfaithfulness (v. 6-43)

The Psalmist shifts into the main part of this Psalm, a larger community lament that ultimately serves to highlight God’s faithfulness.

He starts this by confessing that the “modern” Israel is culpable for their sins just as their fathers are. They are to blame for their iniquity, their wickedness, just as much as their fathers are. Essentially the Psalmist says, “We are no different from the scenes I’m about to show. We’re no better than they are.”

## Picture 1: Rebellion at the Red Sea (v. 6-12)

> [[Bible/ESV/Exodus/Exod 14#11|Exodus 14:11-12]]
> [*11] They said to Moses, “Is it because there are no graves in Egypt that you have taken us away to die in the wilderness? What have you done to us in bringing us out of Egypt? [12] Is not this what we said to you in Egypt: ‘Leave us alone that we may serve the Egyptians’? For it would have been better for us to serve the Egyptians than to die in the wilderness.”\*

A definitive picture of unfaithfulness. The Israelites do _not_ hope in the Lord, but are instead sure they will die.

The Psalmist show’s how God contrasts this, with the word, “yet.” The Lord saves them, so that they may see his power, his glory.

v.9 - Look how powerful he is, not a drop, not a puddle in there way. It is “like a desert.”

> [!question] ❓
> Why does the Psalmist include this detail?
> _Show how complete God’s power is. They don’t step through puddles, the Lord turns the bottom of the sea, the wettest, muddiest spot, bone dry for his people. _

We see here that the Lord, when he seeks to make his mighty power known, does not reserve it. He provides _abundantly_ for his people even despite their doubts (unfaithfulness) to God.

The Psalmist continues, by showing us just as the way was made dry, so can it be undone and it is done so magnificently. Not an enemy is left when the waters of the red sea close.

> [!question] ❓
> What is the response of Israel to this? Is it good or bad?
> \*Good! They \***\*believe\*\*\*\*\***\*and they \*\*\*\*sing \*_\*\*(v.12)_

> [!question] ❓
> Why is this response good?
> _They acknowledge God’s mighty work and seek to praise him, give him thanks, for the work he has done for them._

So we close this picture with a not so bad response. **Here Israel proves faithful, because they turn to God.** There is a bit of doubt, rebellion, at the Sea, but the people still acknowledge God. Nice!**Transition**

As we have the background singing of Israel fading out, the metaphorical instrumental music continues in a soaring major key and then suddenly changes to minor key. The Israelites have done something wrong… Verse 13 starts with a contrasting word, “but!” They’ve done the opposite of believing and singing praise!

Instead they have forgotten God’s works (all the plagues in Egypt and the work in the Red Sea) and worse still, they did not wait for the Lord to give counsel.

Instead, the Israelites give into…

## Picture 4: Unpleasant People in a Pleasant Land (v. 34-42)

_(Joshua - Judges)_

The Psalmist moves into the lowest portion of the Psalm, the time between the end of Joshua and throughout Judges. None of the scenes are extremely specific here, but we should call out the outright wickedness of the people in this section.

**They serve idols**, again look back to the episode with Baal. They have replaced God, but the Psalmist shows just how backward they are!** They sacrifice their children**, rather than sacrifice animals to the living God as atonement for sin. They accumulate sin by offering humans to a fake god.

This is the lowest part Israel hits!

The Lord must punish this evil and he does. He gives his people over to other nations. This in turn causes Israel to realize their sin, v. 43 says they are brought low in their iniquity (sin). It’s when they are in this low state they realize their need for the Lord!

## Persistence of God’s Love (v. 43-48)

However, despite all of this God delivers them.

If go back once more to the illustration of music, the orchestra has been playing in a minor key for _some time_ now. However, there’s a bit of silence and we hear it pick up with a major key once more. Small and quiet at first in 43, but swelling into 44-46.

### **In the Past (v. 43-46)** Despite *all *the sin, God delivers them.

There are some interesting contrasts here:

7. Where Israel was **deaf** to God’s admonishment, God**hears** their cry of distress
8. While Israel had **forgot** God (v.21). God**remembered** them, and his covenant.
9. When Israel twice **whored** after other gods. God had**steadfast love** that could not be broken.

God had every reason to wipe Israel away, yet he shows them love. Steadfast love.

**God is faithful, where Israel is unfaithful.**

God does not walk away even from a rebellious people.

### **In the Present (v. 47)**

The Psalmist knows this characteristic of God, his faithfulness, as he’s spent a _lot_ of time writing about it. And he invokes it God’s faithfulness once more for the present situation Israel is in. We’re not told in the title when the Psalm is written, but we do know that he asks God to once again deliver his people so that they may gather in worship to God

### **In the Future (v. 47)**

The Psalmist knows this characteristic will continue! He calls for the Lord to be blessed and brings up that familiar phrase, “everlasting to everlasting.”

Bless the Lord from eternity past to eternity future! He is faithful from the beginning of time to the end and we give praise to him for this quality!
