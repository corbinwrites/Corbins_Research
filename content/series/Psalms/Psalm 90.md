_Praise, Lament, Request_

_Corporate Lament_

3. **If known, what is the historical context for this psalm?**

_This is possibly at the end of Moses life, [[Bible/ESV/Deuteronomy/Deut 33|Deut 33]]._

_References to: Genesis, “Before the mountains were brought forth” which he had most likely written_

_God’s Patient Love Despite Israel’s faults _“Grass renewed in the morning, even though it fades.”

_God’s righteous anger, “We are brought to an end by your anger” he had definitely seen that God’s holiness, “You have set our inquiries before you.”_

_Wandering in the Wilderness, “For as many days as you have afflicted us”_

4. **Who are the subjects within the psalm?**

_Israel - “our”, The LORD_

5. **What is the intent of this psalm? How would it be used? \***It reads as a reflective, lament meant for Israel to sing and think on. A wisened Moses looks to the people and reminds them of the good, powerful God they serve.\*6.**How does singing this in corporate worship shape the hearts of the covenant people?**

_It humbles them. You are dust. You have a great and powerful God who serves you._

Major Motifs:

- Time - 19 mentions of a time of day, the span of time, time beginning, or time ending
- Death - 6 mentions of the “end of time”, death

Structure is SO cool

A positivity sandwich starting with praise for God, getting to the middle with lament, and ending with supplication and favor

What’s this Psalm about?

A Psalm around how time is short, but our God is eternal.

**Purpose:** Acknowledge the short length of our lives, humbly submit those limited days to God and Rejoice in the eternity of God**Outline**

7. Eternal God (V. 1-2) - Praise
8. Temporary Man (V. 3-11) - Lament
9. Humble Request (v. 12-17)

**Historical Context**

Moses is writing this after they have seen the promised land the first time and are in the 40 years of wandering, it’s possible it was after a major event (the snakes biting people) or the pillar of smoke and flame leaving the people (due to v. 13-14). Collins, and I, would place this late. I believe [[Bible/ESV/Deuteronomy/Deut 33|Deut 33]], a final lesson from Moses, inspired by the Spirit, to the people of Israel.

**Eternal God**(V. 1-2) - Praise

- Dwelling place - a place of refuge, a home
- He was the refuge, even before creation. From everlasting, eternity past, to everlasting, eternity to come.
- No matter the place in time he holds the title, God.

**Temporary Man** (V. 3-11) - Lament

_Dust in the Wind _(v. 3-6)

- In sharp contrast is man, who is dust. Swept away in a moment.
- The scale of time for God to man is so sharply different. A thousands years pass for us with what is but a few hours for God.
- _Moses uses a few descriptions here to describes man’s temporary nature. How did you interpret them?_
  - _A flood_
  - _A dream_
  - _Grass that renews and withers_
- **_God wipes away man._**
  - We know this is man because of the use of the word “for” in verse 4. It functions like a bracket, an explanation of verse 3. Verse 5 returns to the original subject, which is man. We prove man by the use of the third simile in v. 6 which speaks to how man is renewed, but fades. A quality that would better suit man than a year.

_God’s anger and wrath_ (v. 7-9)

Key point! We are deep in OT! We are very much in a time of communal blessing and communal cursing. We see that in **Leviticus 26**

- When Moses writes things like, “we are brought to an end of by your anger” it has an eye to the disobedience of the nation. It could also have an eye to what John MacArthur says is that we’re brought to an end due to the angry judgement of God against sin. Our flesh and the curse of Adam is our condemnation. In either case, It’s a fair, righteous anger and wrath that rightly deserves terror.
- ESPECIALLY in the context of the next verse. The Lord reveals sins before him. This gives further credence to the idea that this is judgement.

_Return oh Lord!_

They ask for his satisfaction with his love, rather than their own way

_Why does Moses say the ends of life is “like a sigh?”_

“Who considers the power of your anger, according to the fear of you?”

- Rhetorical question.
- Who can know the full power of God’s anger as the fear demands? We never will

know God’s full power, at least in our current state, probably never.

**Humble Request**(v. 12-17)

- With an understanding that we cannot make sense of his power, that we are weak, feeble, and dust. Moses transitions to what makes sense, supplication for the Lord to lend his power to his people, so that they may have a significant life
  - A heart of wisdom
    - _What does it mean to number our days?_
    - Reminiscent to what Solomon would later write in **Ecclesiastes 7:4**
      - _“The heart of the wise is in the house of mourning, but the heart of fools is in the house of mirth.”_ There’s more wisdom in a somber state of mind.
  - Satisfaction in the morning
  - Joy all our days
  - Favor upon us
  - Establish the work
- v. 16 points the effort of this work to the right place. The lords work is shown to this generation, so that his power is shown to generations to come. Moses, rightly attributes this power to God, and prays that the work they do now will glorify him.
  - This concept for us as believers, finds its parallel in “Whatever you do, do all to the glory of God” (1 Cor. 10:31)

**Application Question**

10. _This Psalm reminds us that we are a flash-in-the pan? How does that make you want to live your life?_
11. _How do we live as if we are numbering our days? What does it look like in your life to do that? Practically. How does that influence your faith walk?_
12. _With a mindset of [[Bible/ESV/1 Corinthians/1 Cor 10#31|1 Cor 10:31]], where we we seek to glorify God in all things, is there a request that Moses makes that we need to ask for from God as well?_
    - _Number our Days - Use Time Wisely_
    - _Satisfaction with his love_
    - _Glad/Joy throughout life’s “toil and trouble”_
    - _That we see God’s hand in our life_
    - _Establish the work of our hands_
