### Thoughts on Lament

Lament is an act of worship. The Psalmist here worships the Lord through his lament.

By God’s providence I was listening to a podcast today and the topic was on pastoral lament and the host had said something that struck me.

> _“I believe that sadness is a healing emotion. Fear’s not, anger’s not. Sadness uniquely creates healing in the soul in a fallen world and that’s part of God’s plan.” _

The hosts go on to talk about the prophetic description of Jesus in [[Bible/ESV/Isaiah/Isa 53#3|Isaiah 53:3]]

> **[[Bible/ESV/Isaiah/Isa 53#3|Isaiah 53:3-5]]**> 3 He was despised and rejected by men,** a man of sorrows and acquainted with grief**; and as one from whom men hide their faces he was despised, and we esteemed him not. 4 Surely he has borne our griefs and carried our sorrows; yet we esteemed him stricken, smitten by God, and afflicted. 5 But he was pierced for our transgressions; he was crushed for our iniquities; upon him was the chastisement that brought us peace, and with his wounds we are healed.

And in the New Testament we see this plainly. Even Jesus, by his own admission, is a man stricken with sorrow. For example when he is in the garden of Gethsemane he says,

> **[[Bible/ESV/Matthew/Matt 26#36|Matthew 26:36-45]]
> **36 Then Jesus went with them to a place called Gethsemane, and he said to his disciples, "Sit here, while I go over there and pray." 37 And taking with him Peter and the two sons of Zebedee, he began to be sorrowful and troubled. 38 Then he said to them, "** My soul is very sorrowful, even to death; remain here, and watch with me.**" 39 And going a little farther he fell on his face and prayed, saying, "My Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me; nevertheless, not as I will, but as you will."

As the hosts of these podcasts wisely noted, sorrow is not only a good emotion, but a _christlike_ attribute. One that can be turned into worship to God that expresses our dependence on him.

### Thoughts on “the afflicted one”

However, one of the attributes of this Psalm is that **there is no praise**. This a psalm to sing when are so grieved/afflicted that we can’t declare joy or praise.

<!-- Column 1 -->

He _does not_:

- Say God is wrong
- Know what causes this
- Say God’s face has turned away
- Ask for forgiveness
- Ask for freedom from the affliction

<!-- Column 2 -->

He _does_:

- Refer to God as his salvation (v. 1)
- Say God is the reason for the affliction (v. 6-8)
- Say his affliction has been ongoing for a long time, since his youth (v.15)
- Say God’s face hidden (v. 14)
- Call upon the Lord _frequently_ (v. 1,9,13)
- Express frustration with God (v. 10-12)

**Despite his misery, his faith does not waiver**. This is implicit throughout the entire Psalm. Note that he carries up his prayer to God three times throughout the Psalm. We confidently know that he implicitly trusts the Lord.** If he had no faith, why would he even pray,** let alone pray “day and night?”

# **Outline**

## First Cry to the Lord: Troubled Soul (v. 1-9)

Charles Spurgeon says the title “God of my Salvation” is

> _“\***\*the only ray of comfortable light which shines throughout the psalm.\*\*** The writer has salvation, he is sure of that, and God is the sole author of it. While a person can see God as his Saviour, it is not altogether midnight with him._

I’d agree with him. This is a key phrase that Heman includes for us to anchor our perspective on the Psalm to follow. We know the Psalmist recognizes the Lord as his sole (soul) savior and we must keep that in mind as we go into the depths of his lament.

His first lament is on how his **soul is troubled.**

> [!question] ❓
> What is the Psalmist’s soul troubled by?
> _He is worried about a physical death, but fears more, a condemning death where he is separated from God._

We see this in the use of the words like Sheol, pit, grave and dead, but that could indicate just a physical death in a earthly grave. However, verse 5 takes a turn that shows the harsher death the Psalmist is troubled by:

> _like those whom you remember no more,
> for they are cut off from your hand_

This is not just an earthly death, but spiritual exile. The psalmist would be cutoff from God.

> [!question] ❓
> Is this condemning death a reality for the Psalmist?
> _No, it is his intense feelings coming through_

Note the use of simile in verse 5. He is _like_: One set loose…, the slain… those whom you remember no more…

We must keep in mind verse 1. He attributes God as his salvation.

> [!question] ❓
> The Psalmist, ironically, fears a spiritually condemning death in exile to God. However, we know he has salvation from God. What does this teach us?
> _It’s relatable. There are points in our life where we are so embroiled in sadness, guilt that we feel we will be cast away to the outer darkness. Yes, by bringing that feeling to the Lord, we strengthen our faith._

Charles Spurgeon says,

> _“His distress had not blown out the sparks of his prayer, but quickened them till they burned perpetually like a furnace at full blast.”_

His confession of fear of condemnation does not blow out the small sparks of his faith. Instead, by carrying it to the Lord, he _will_ restore his faith to a burning hot intensity once more.

That being said, this afflicted man turns his gaze from his own sorry state to the creator of his affliction, God.

He first begins with metaphor. It’s non-specific, but they are factually what he is feeling. In the dark, overwhelmed. We are given a sense of despair, perhaps even frustration with God.

**Selah** This afflicted man stops for a moment to gather himself, before he gets more personal.

He cries out, God you have even taken away my friends. I’m appalling to them. Grotesque.

He cries so much, it is hard for him to see. Interesting use of the word, “dim.” As though the light of the Lord has faded to him in his sorrow.

## Second Cry to the Lord: Do they dead praise you? (v. 9-12)

The Psalmist carries his second cry to the Lord. He contemplates this idea of death further and asks God,

“If I die, can you still work a wonder? Can I praise you if I’m dead?”

He takes a pause, a breath, before asks the harder, scarier question.

“Lord, in Hell, can anyone declare your love?”

“In the darkness, can they see your wonder?”

“In forgetfulness, can they remember you?”

The answer is emphatically, no.

If the psalmist is killed and cast away by the Lord he will not experience God’s love, light, or faithfulness. Instead he would experience wrath, darkness, and no memory of God’s righteousness.

> [!question] ❓
> There is something the Psalmist is implicitly fearing here. What is it?
> _Loss of salvation_

Yes, again we return to that theme. The Psalmist, ironically, is fearful of losing salvation. Why is it ironic, because he believes in the Lord and faithfully carries his fears to Him.

What a model for us all!

## Third Cry to the Lord: I continue to pray but feel no reply (v.13-18)

The psalmist continues to the final cry.

He starts with, “but.” He contrasts the very point he just made. Almost as though this afflicted man says, “Yes Lord, this is my fear, but instead I _come to you in prayer_.” He does so with fervor. He rises in the morning so that before his day and troubles begin once more he can carry his prayer, his fears, to the Lord.

As we’ve read in many Psalms, we expect this ending to be filled with praise, but instead it continues in despair, repeating many similar ideas to the first cry.

We see again:

- Fear he has lost salvation
- Water imagery crushing down on him
- God’s wrath poured on him
- His friends leaving him

The new information we gain is that he has had this affliction since he was a child. It’s possible it could be a disease, it’s possible it could be something else. Either way he’s fought this for a long time. Perhaps he has fears that this affliction is punishment for a sin he doesn’t know. The Pharisees ask this of Jesus. Perhaps this has lead to the fear of his salvation? We don’t know.

We do know that even at the close of this Psalm, this afflicted man is not encouraged _just yet_.

He is still sad. He is still grieving. He is still mourning. However, we can take hope that he will continue to take his prayer, and hope, to the Lord every day and night. While he may *feel *his salvation is lost, he will continue to secure his salvation by humbly submitting to God his fears and troubles through prayer. This in turn, will continue to build a personal, lasting connection with God.
