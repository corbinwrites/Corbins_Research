**What kind of psalm is this?**

Thanksgiving

**If known, what is the historical context for this psalm?**

No context for when it was written, but the context of when it was sung is known. Part of the Hallel “Praise” Psalms (113-118). [[Bible/ESV/Psalms/Ps 114|Psalm 114]] has a mention to exodus, so they are all traditionally sung at passover.

**Who are the subjects within the psalm?**

Me - Psalmist/Singer; Him, You - God

**What is the intent of this psalm? How would it be used?**

A song of praise to thank the Lord for how he has saved him from physical and spiritual death. This would be used as a reminder and praise for the Lord delivering a singer from a trial.

**How does singing this in corporate worship shape the hearts of the covenant people?**

A reminder of how the Lord has continued to protect and care for us. Within a corporate setting it’s a stirring reminder of how God has done the same to us. According to Collins, this was sometimes sung for new mothers.

# Lesson

As I’ve been reading this, this Psalmist comes across like Kramer from Seinfield. He bursts into the bible with lots of words and it’s just from the heart. Thankfully, this psalmist’s heart is filled with love, but that means the structure is super loose.

## **Praise (v. 1-11)\*\***Praise Sandwich (v. 1-9)\*\*

I’m going to skip around a bit in this first part, so prepare yourself.

v.1 - The Psalmist begins by proclaiming his love for the Lord because He (the Lord) has heard his voice and plea.

> [!question] ❓
> What is his plea for mercy?
> \*v. 3-4 *What did the psalmist ask for mercy from?
> *Physical death, perhaps
> Certainly spiritual harm! He asks for the Lord to deliver his soul\*Yes, the psalmist’s pleas for mercy from death (spiritual and physical) were heard by they Lord. In verse 2, he expounds how the Lord heard him and the psalmist says that the Lord**inclined** his ear to him.

> [!question] ❓
> What does inclined mean?
> _Literally in latin inclinare - to bend towards. God doesn’t just hear this prayer, he’s attentively listening. Focusing on listening. _

Not only has the Lord spared his life, but God has so attentively heard his plea. He didn’t passively wait for a small shout, but was focusing on listening to the Psalmist.

Since we talked 3-4, i’m going to skip down to 5. Where we get to the next piece of praise bread. Having talked about the affliction he suffered the Psalmist once more praises the Lord.

> [!question] ❓
> Define gracious - show favor or esteem. You would do this to a significant person! But it is undeserved in our case!
> Define righteous - a correct fair judge according to the covenant he had set before his people
> Define merciful - a “brotherly/motherly feeling” compassion an affective aspect to love. _Maraham_

He displays these characteristics in the preservation of the simple

> [!question] ❓
> What does he mean by simple?
> _Those who are plain, those without incredible talent or skill. _

> _“In Psalms and Proverbs the “simple” are not firmly committed either to wisdom or to folly but are easily misled (cf. comment on 19:7–11; cf. Ps. 119:130; Prov. 1:4). That is, they are Israelites who have not fully laid hold of God’s grace—and yet the Lord in his generous love cares even for them.”_
>
> _Excerpt From
> ESV Expository Commentary: Psalms–Song of Solomon_

The Psalmist says this to draw a connection to himself with the simple. Here the Lord preserved him. He was brought low (low enough to be at Sheol) and was saved.

The Psalmist commands his soul to return to rest for the Lord has been generous. Which is beautiful. There is a point in our life where we are resting in the Lord and we can return to it.

> “Whenever a child of God even for a moment loses his peace of mind, he should be concerned to find it again, not by seeking it in the world or in his own experience, but in the Lord alone. When the believer prays, and the Lord inclines his ear, the road to the old rest is before him. For the Lord hath dealt bountifully with thee. You have served a good God, and built upon a sure foundation; come back to him who in former days condescended to enrich you by his love”

Excerpt From
Psalms Volume II
Charles Spurgeon

There is rest beside the Lord, just like we read last week.

The psalmist continues his praise in this interesting staircase word picture in verses 8-9:

- God saves his soul from death, down in Sheol, the deeps
- God clears the tears from his child, image of someone laying down
- God then gets his child to his feet, and steadies him
- We see the psalmist walking on his own two legs and he is walking in the Land of the living in front of God.

I think the psalmist just means this practically too. By delivering him, the Lord has done each of these things. He’s made it so the Psalmist can walk the road to righteousness.

**Confession v.10-11**

Admittedly the structure breaks up here. The psalmist in this outpouring of love for God stops in this interesting moment of confession and teaching.

> [!question] ❓
> What stands out to you in Verses 10-11?
> _He believed despite it all.
> Even though he was ‘Greatly afflicted’
> Even though he exaggerated but said ‘All mankind are liars.”_

Despite all the hardship he continued to believe. A lesson to us as well! Even in our trial, so should we follow this example and believe!

## Payment**(v. 12-19)**

From all of this, the Psalmist wants to repay the Lord for what he’s done (so much so he says it twice). However, this section opens up with a rhetorical question,

He fully acknowledges that he certainly cannot repay the Lord. However, this doesn’t stop him from attempting to! An excellent model for us even as New Testament believers.

He starts by saying he will lift the cup of salvation and call on the name of the Lord.

> [!question] ❓
> What is the cup of salvation?

**Physical**

> Excerpt From

>
> **Metaphorical**

> [!question] ❓
> What does it mean to “pay my vows in the presence of all people”?

These acts of worship focus especially on the peace offering. There are five broad categories of offerings in the Pentateuch, each with its own functions. The peace offering is the only one from which the ordinary worshiper can eat. Gordon Wenham describes it in this way:

\*In all the other sacrifices the worshipper received nothing back, but in the peace offering most of the flesh was shared out by the worshipper with his family and friends, thus making the sacrificial meal a joyful barbecue. . . .
What was acted out in this sacrifice was the believer’s daily experience: God had given him life and health initially, and every day in grace renewed these gifts. In every sacrifice the worshipper gave himself back to God in penitence and commitment, and in return received back a renewal of physical and spiritual life. Usually however there was no immediately visible sign of this renewal of life, but in the peace offering there was. The shared luxury of a meat meal was a tangible, indeed[…]”

\*Excerpt From
ESV Expository Commentary: Psalms–Song of Solomon
John Collins

He interjects his appeals to sing yet more praise to God. He remembers that he almost died again. He acknowledges that God does not take the death of his saints lightly. They are precious, a depiction of his tenderness. They are of value to God like precious gems.

God’s tenderness continues as he has has allowed the Psalmist to be his servant and loosed the psalmist’s bonds.

> [!question] ❓
> What was the psalmist bound to?
> _Sin, Death, Sheol_

> “Thou hast loosed my bonds - freedom from bondage binds me to thy service. He who is loosed from the bonds of sin, death, and hell should rejoice to wear the easy yoke of the great Deliverer. It ought to create rapture in our souls if we are able to call Jesus Master, and are acknowledged by him as his servants.”
> Excerpt From
> Psalms Volume II
> Charles Spurgeon

The Psalmist is no longer bound to death but instead is in servanthood to the true King.

With this section of praise complete, the psalmist repeats 13-14 once more in 17-18 with a small variation.

The Psalmist is **serious about** this. He really wants to offer to the Lord a peace offering. He wants to sacrifice to the Lord for the work that God has done in his life.

And that’s the first question I want you to all think about tonight.

Do you love God so much that you want to publicly sacrifice for him? It will look a little different a New Testament christian, but what does a thanksgiving sacrifice look like for you?
