**[[Bible/ESV/Psalms/Ps 1|Psalm 1]] Teaching Notes**

_What’s the single meaning of this Psalm?_

_How many sections did you all think this Psalm could be broken into?_

C. John Collins says:

I.  Contrasting Sources of Values (1:1–2)

II.  Contrasting Fruitfulness (1:3–4)

III.  Contrasting Outcomes of Their Lives (1:5–6)

**This Psalm can be broken into four parts**

1. Define the Characters (Blessed/Wicked) (v. 1-2)
2. Result of the Blessed (v. 3)
3. Result of the Wicked (v. 4-5)
4. Resolution (v. 6)

**Define the Characters (Righteous/Wicked) (v. 1-2)**

- Verse 1 defines what a blessed _person_ is
- **Define “Blessed?”** - “Deep seated joy and contentment with God.”
  - We’ll develop this blessing in verse 3. That section helps give us a figurative definition for this word, “blessed.”
  - Someone who does not take counsel from wicked, stands alongside sinners, and scoffs.
  - The imagery of walking, standing, and sitting is very interesting here. _Why do you think the Psalmist uses that imagery?_
    - Charles Spurgeon had some helpful thoughts here:
      - “When people are living in sin they go from bad to worse. At first **they merely walk in the counsel of the careless and ungodly**, who forget God - the evil is rather practical than habitual - but after that they become habituated to evil, and **they stand in the way of open sinners who willfully violate God's commandments**; and if let alone, they go one step further, and become themselves pestilent teachers and** tempters of others**, and thus **they sit in the seat of the scornful**. They have taken their degree in vice, and as true Doctors of Damnation they are installed, and are looked up to by others as Masters in Satan. But the blessed man, the man to whom all the blessings of God belong, can hold no communion with such characters as these.”
  - Note: the very helpful contrasting word here: “but!”
    - So we know a blessed person does _not_ do the previously mentioned things. Instead this person will
      - **Delight** in the law of the Lord and **meditate** on it.
        - Yes, even Chronicles!
        - That could look like:
          - Scripture memorization
          - Reading regularly/Bible Reading plan
          - Singing songs from the Bible
          - Praying through verses of the Bible
          - Journaling on verses
          - Teaching/Counseling with the Bible
        - No matter what you do though, find **JOY** in it. The Psalmist says it’s the blessed person’s delight! It is the emotional fire that warms him in the midst of the spiritual winter of this world.
- So to recap!
  - Blessed: Not “one of the sinners”, delights in the Bible, thinks on it often
  - Wicked: In “crowd of sinners”, would not delight in the Bible, or think of it often

**Result of the Blessed (v. 3)**

_Why do you think the Psalmist uses these images?_

**_To describe how someone is blessed_**

- “Planted by streams of water” - Continually refreshed, never parched.
  - Has the resource needed to grow big and strong! With only soil and sun, this tree would not be able to grow to full size, but with a stream continually refreshing it, the tree can grow large and strong.
  - Just like a river, your bible is always ready to be tapped into and refreshing. Note the Psalmist says there are plural streams/rivers. There is a chance a single river could run dry, but it would be difficult for multiple rivers to run dry. Similarly, the Bible will not run dry for you.
- “Yields fruit”
  - Reliable! It has apples during apple season.
  - Fruits of the spirit. This person exemplifies the attributes of God that we can embody as limited beings. Love, Patience, Kindness, Self-Control. _Give me the rest(?)_
  - Gifts for others. Continually generous. Uses talents for others.
- “Leaf does not wither”
  - Strong! Hardy. Persistent. Can hold up to the hot sun and cold winter. Whenever this person walks into a time conflict, anxiousness, fear, etc. and they know God’s word, they will not wither at the conflict. Instead they are strong and hardy! They know the truth of God’s word and hopefully feel assurance in that time.

**Result of the Wicked (v. 4-5)**

- “No, not the wicked.” The Psalmist says, yeah all that stuff I just said, doesn’t fly for the wicked.
- Instead they are driven away, like the outside of wheat. Threshed in the air until it falls away. Beat the devil out the stalks of wheat to do it, then the chaff scatters to the wind. Not in a single location just out and about.
- We see that is indeed the case in verse 4 with synonymous parallelism. The wicket will not gather in two places, in judgement and the congregation
  - **Judgement** can refer to discipline or corrective behavior. That’s a reasonable interpretation, implying that the wicked would not even be corrected due to the fact that they don’t recognize the authority of God.
  - I’d say a better understanding would be final judgement, as we talked about yesterday with Chris. One depiction in the new testament as the separating of the goats from the sheep (Matt 25:31-46).
    - While we recognize that all will be judged, it comes across as a hyperbole. Like the Psalmist is saying, the wicked are so bad, they don’t even deserve to be judged! They won’t even have the privilege of coming into the court room.
  - Synonymously, the writer says sinners (read as wicked), won’t get to hang out with the righteous. Again, this is further affirmed with final judgement language Jesus uses in the New Testament.

**Resolution (v. 6)**

- The Lord knows “with affection and approval” similar to the word “prefers.” (Amos 3:2, [[Bible/ESV/Genesis/Gen 18#19|Gen 18:19]]). According to C. John Collins. Spurgeon says, “[God] is constantly looking on their way and thought it may be often [to us] in mist and darkness, yet the Lord knows it.”
- In stark contrast is the wicked, which is plainly told to us that they will die. Yet not only them, but their way will die.

“Not only will they perish themselves, but their way will perish too. The righteous carves his name upon the rock, but the wicked writes his remembrance in the sand. The righteous man plows the furrows of earth, and sows a harvest here which will never be fully reaped till he enters the enjoyments of eternity; but as for the wicked, he plows the sea, and though there may seem to be a shining trail behind his keel, yet the waves will pass over it, and the place that knew him will know him no more forever.” - Charles Spurgeon

**Application:**

5. _Do you delight in the law of the Lord? How can you grow your delight for it?_
6. _How will you personally meditate on the law of the Lord? How can you do so “day and night?”_
7. _Are you like the tree? What can you do to build those attributes of the tree? Growing, Fruitful, and Hardy._
