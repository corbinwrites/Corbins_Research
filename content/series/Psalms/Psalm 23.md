# Lesson

Purpose: Come to a new appreciation of God’s abundant care and generosity that he displays for his people.

## The Shepherd and the Sheep

**Verse 1**

> [!question] ❓
> What does it mean for the Lord to be a “shepherd?”
> Protects, provides, cleans, cares, searches, holds, feeds, guides the sheep

> [!question] ❓
> What does it mean for David/the reader to be a “sheep?”
> In need of care, dumb, get lost, hungry, selfish

These are all great answers, but I want to make sure we highlight, underline, put in bold. Who our shepherd is. The LORD, Yahweh, is our shepherd.

Yahweh, creator of the world, defender of Israel, the one who parted the Red Sea, the one who led Israel by fire and flame. Who’s glory is so magnificent that Moses needed to be shut into a rock just to see the backside of God as he walked by. This most powerful being who is omniscient, omnipotent, and omnipresent is described as our caretaker. One who personally tends to us like a humble shepherd looking after his sheep.

It goes with out saying, given the qualities I just listed that the sheep in his care shall not want. All their needs are met and provided. A point of application for us to ponder later in our groups, do we believe that? Do we see that the shepherd has provided for all our _wants_?

**Verse 2**

David gives us two illustrations to expand how God meets our wants.

> [!question] ❓
> Look at verse 2, interpret the passage. Some questions to consider:
> **1. What is the significance of green pastures?**
> _A place to be fed. Spurgeon writes that this is the word of God. The sheep can feast on his words. _
> **2. What is the significance of still waters?**
> _A place to drink fully. The waters are calm, not treacherous. _
> **3. What does the posture of the sheep teach us? \***”lie down” the sheep can sit and rest for a long while eating here
> ”lead me” the shepherd is in arms reach\*

These images can be summarized neatly in the first part of verse 3. **Restoration.** Though, I think what’s important to note is from **where the restoration springs from.** Not from the green pastures and still waters, no it comes from the LORD.

“**He** restores my soul.”

**The restoration of our heart can only come from God**. As new testament believers, we know this to be believe and faith in the life, death and resurrection of Jesus the Messiah. As old testament believers, this was hope in the Messiah to come. In both circumstances, it is _this_ faith that restores us. Certainly, spiritual disciplines like bible reading, prayer, meditation on the things above are essential to the faith, but they must be done in a spirit that is restored.

**Verse 3**

Let’s return to **verse 3** to see the shepherd who has led us to green pastures and still waters is leading us somewhere else, paths of righteousness.

> [!question] ❓
> What does David mean by “paths of righteousness?”
> \*Right-living, uprightness, the Christian walk

*Not a reading comprehension question: Is the “path of righteousness” good for us? Why?
*Yes
_[_[[Bible/ESV/Psalms/Ps 16#11|Psalm 16:11]]_](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm%2016%3A11&version=ESV)_ You make known to me the path of life; in your presence there is fullness of joy; at your right hand are pleasures forevermore.\*

We know this path is good for us, as we’ve explored, but we see **the shepherd leads us on the path for \*\*\***his **\***name’s sake**. Note that t**he shepherd is walking in front of us here.\*\* He is showing us the way to walk, where to navigate. The shepherd steps to the right to stay on the path, the sheep takes a right as well. He steps left to stay on the path and avoid a thicket that has grown off the side of the path, the sheep follows and also avoids the barbed stems of the bush. The sheep takes the same steps as the shepherd and easily follows Him down the trail

But **what if we don’t follow the shepherd.** He stays on the path and walks around the thicket and we plunge headfirst into it. The LORD has continued to lead us well, but alas, we did not heed our Shepherd’s instruction. The shepherd is not bad or less caring, but **we have selfishly proceeded with our own plan.**

I mention the opposite of this verse so that we may have it in mind when we encounter this phrase at the end of verse 3 ]where it says he leads us for, “_his name’s sake.”_ **Which essentially means that he leads us for\*\*\*** his**\*** glory, not our glory.\*\* Let’s say this sheep follows the shepherd excellently. This this sheep would be well-fed, restored, and fully reliant on the shepherd. This would ascribe glory to the shepherd! Look how healthy and good the sheep is! This because the sheep is reliant on his shepherd and the shepherd so excellent leads and tends the sheep.

However, imagine if the sheep followed the shepherd faithfully, avoiding every root and hole and instead this sheep was bedraggled, malnourished, injured. Despite the sheep’s careful steps, this sheep looks like he has been through every thicket. This would mean that the sheep has a** bad shepherd**. One who does not care for the sheep well. We would **not glorify **this shepherd, because he has done a bad job. Thankfully our shepherd is not bad, but good, let me review:

So all that to say:

14. The Lord/Shepherd leads us on the path of righteousness
15. This path and the shepherd makes us good healthy sheep
16. By being good healthy sheep, we are a testament to how good of a shepherd he is
17. This brings glory to him

**Verse 4**

While this path may have some small hinderances off the side of the path, it’s manageable. However, as we follow the Lord we see that this righteous path gets a little scarier. A little darker. The path leads the sheep and shepherd through a dark valley. Note in the journey presented here that the path of righteousness has lead us here and **we must go through this valley.**

> [!question] ❓
> If Christian life is the path of righteousness, what could be the “valley of the shadow of death?”
> _Literally death, hardship, illness, trial_

Note that the timid sheep who is reliant on the Lord **does not fear as he enters the valley**.

Spurgeon points out that the sheep _walks_ through the valley,

> “Yea, though I walk,” as if the believer did not quicken his pace when he came to die, but still calmly walked with God. To walk indicates the steady advance of a soul which knows its road, knows its end, resolves to follow the path, feels quite safe, and is therefore perfectly calm and composed.”
> Psalms Volume I
> Charles Spurgeon

Commentator David Gibson expands on Spurgeon’s point in Lord of [[Bible/ESV/Psalms/Ps 23|Psalm 23]], to point out that **the shepherd’s position has shifted.** **He is no longer \*\*\***leading**\*** but instead walking **\***alongside**\***. \*\*

“You are with me.”

The confidence of the sheep is, yes, in the hope to come, as Spurgeon says, but also in the shepherd who is actively walking alongside through the valley fraught with peril.

A final note on this simple phrase, “You are with me.” is line 10, is the exact middle of the Psalm. This line is the finality, the main point of all the generosity and restoration that is depicted in this Psalm. **The greatest gift, is God’s living, active presence with us at all times, especially in the valley of darkness.**

David points out that this shepherd has not arrived to the valley empty-handed though. He has with him two instruments to ensure the comfort of the sheep, the rod and the staff.

> [!question] ❓
> What’s a rod and staff?
> \*Rod is essentially a mace. Big stick with a weighted end to club predators. The same word is used in [[Bible/ESV/Isaiah/Isa 10#5|Isaiah 10:5-8]] “Woe to Assyria the rod of my anger; the staff in their hands is my fury!”

Staff is the classic shepherd’s crook. It would lead, direct the sheep. Used to pull the sheep out of whatever they were tangled in and keep them on track.\*

I enjoyed the description Gibson gives of the shepherd walking through this valley with us. He has **a two-handed mace, muscles-flexed read to club any opponent who harms his sheep.** This does give great comfort. The Lord will seek to defend us from wolves. He must do this for us daily, I know not how many predators he has crushed around me and I think that’s the point. I’ll not spend much time on the rod.

**The staff **is the gentle direction and guidance we need as we walk through the trial. We can be excited by the club coming down on a wolf as its jaws snap at us, but **annoyed when we feel the gentle tap of the crook on our side**. This **staff is an expression of love, but can be delivered in the form of correction (exhortation and admonishment)**. The sheep may be headed for thicket in the Valley of darkness, and the shepherd will attempt to correct the sheep’s path. How do you respond to the shepherd’s correction? Do you tug against the crook and head straight for the thicket? Do you submit and follow once more on the path of righteousness?

I should also stop to point out that as a church, we are now the body of Christ with him at the head. **[[Bible/ESV/1 Corinthians/1 Cor 12#27|1 Corinthians 12:27]].** That means correction, may be delivered from a friend, member, or elder of the church (a “hand” of the body). Are we ok when one of them gently pats you with the shepherd’s crook to keep you on the path of righteousness? Or will we be a selfish sheep and walk right into the thorn bush?

## The Host and the Friend

Suddenly, David switches images on us. **The hands of the shepherd no** longer contain a rod and staff, but** oil and wine**. Instead of sheep, we are people once more, and surprise, we are now eating, a favorite image of the Psalms. In this next part, David imagines **God as a host who is caring for a special guest**. I’d argue though that God treats us greater than a guest in these images, instead we are a dear friend. Let’s look.

> [!question] ❓
> What’s the significance of a table being prepared?
> What does that mean for the one eating the feast (the guest)?
> What does that mean for the ones watching (the enemies)?

I so enjoy Gibson’s interpretation of the table prepared, so I shall let him tell it:

**This is no simple taunt to those who have not, but rather concern and abundance for those whom God loves and cares for (those within his flock). **

Worth noting as well, is that it’s not that the the White Witch or the enemies in the Psalm are NOT invited to feast. The table is open, all may enter and share of the meal. **However, to do so means to acknowledge the host and submit to the shepherd’s path. These enemies are unwilling to do so. **

The Lord not only seeks to prepare a feast but also to anoint this special guest.

> [!question] ❓
> What is the significance of anointing?
> _We are tempted to think that this is done to consecrate, make holy and it is! However, the word here is not the anointing of kings and priests (mashakh), instead it literally translates to “make my head fat with oil” (dishshen). Gibson points out that anointing was also used as a respect for a guest. You’d wash their feet and anoint their head. Like taking a guest’s coat. _

**This is a description of the host’s generosity. **The host isn’t just pouring out a little bit of the this fragrant oil, but drenching the guest in it.

If that statement of generosity is unclear, hopefully the next one clarifies. David says that his “cup overflows.” This is the picture of host who is so generous with his pouring of wine that he doesn’t just fill the cup halfway, 3/4, or even to the top. This host is so generous with the drink that he overflows the cup. It pours out on to the table. David drinks the cup and again the host comes again and gives a similar pour. The cup cannot contain the generosity of this host.

> [!question] ❓
> What does the cup symbolize?
> _Our lives!_

Yes, David looking at everything says **the cup of his life cannot contain the generosity of God. **

The next statement builds on that. Talking about this generosity, David looks back to his life to see what is behind him. What is walking behind him? He sees “goodness” and “mercy” not just following, but the better word is **pursue** him. **Goodness and mercy are relentlessly hunting him like a lion chasing a zebra. He cannot escape this goodness and mercy!**

> [!question] ❓
> We know they pursue David and they pursue us, but what is meant by “goodness” and “mercy?”

_“the “goodness” which pursues him is the covenant goodness of God”_
_The goodness of God that provides manna in the wilderness. Who continually provides_

> _“He knows that the “mercy” hot on his heels is the covenant mercy of God: it is hesed, the word for God’s steadfast love (as the ESV footnote records). This is his loving-kindness, his loyal, committed, faithful love.”_

Some commentators talk to this being the “sheepdogs” that bring up the rear of the sheep. Others talk to this like angels, but I most enjoy Gibson’s rendering which is “goodness and mercy” is truly **God. Nothing embodies those qualities better than He. **

When the Sheep looks behind him on the trail, he sees the shepherd now stands behind him, pursuing him. It was the shepherd who had freed the sheep from every snare. It was the shepherd who protected the sheep from wolves and lions. It was the shepherd who tapped him back on to the path of righteousness and the sheep’s conclusion? ALL of his life contained goodness. ALL of his life had God’s mercy. It was God who orchestrated all of this behind him.

Gibson points out, and I will emphasize, **it is possible you are walking through a valley of darkness **at the moment.** You may not be looking back and seeing goodness and mercy due to the threats pressing in around you.** That’s ok. Lean on the strong shepherd who protects you. When you pass through the valley though, I’m sure you will see that your life has been good and if not, you may need to walk further down the path of righteousness to see it.

Finally, We finish with this confident statement, “I, the sheep, will dwell in the house of the Lord forever.” Yes, this is the final destination. “Shall” is the future tense. This path of righteousness will ultimately result in the sheep dwelling, living, resting, in the house of Yahweh, God of all creation. Let us give thanks to him for the fact that he escorts us down the path of righteousness to the this sacred, special home.

## Questions

18. In verse one David says that the shepherd has provided for all his wants. Do you believe the Lord has done that? How have you arrived to that conclusion?
19. In verse 4, the sheep finds comfort in both the rod and the staff. How do you feel about the shepherd’s staff when it corrects you? Do you tug against the crook and head straight for the thicket? Do you submit and follow once more on the path of righteousness? Why?
20. In verse 6, it says that goodness and mercy pursue the sheep. When you look back on your life do you see goodness and mercy pursuing you? Explain your perspective.
