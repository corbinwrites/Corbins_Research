# Lesson

## Awaits the Lord (v. 1-2)

**v. 1 - “** Alone” - Only

For God only does my soul wait in silence.

> [!question] ❓
> What’s the significance of David’s soul waiting in silence?
> _No complaint, there’s anticipation, and eagerness to hear what the Lord will say or see what He will do_

David, rightly attributes his salvation to God. I would argue that _is_ what David is waiting for, his salvation. Certainly in a sense for his soul, but even practically from his bad situation.

**v. 2 -** David says that the Lord is his rock, salvation, a fortress.

> [!question] ❓
> What’s the significance of God being a rock?
> _Rocks are strong foundations! They can’t be lit on fire, or easily destroyed. _

What’s the significance of God being a fortress?
_A place of refuge that can repel any attack. All are safe inside. _

Since the Lord is his refuge, his protection, David says he will not be shaken.

## **Co** nfronts the Enemy (v. 3-4)**v. 3 -** speaking of

> [!question] ❓
> What is meant of like leaning wall, a tottering fence? What is the subject that is “like” this?

**Option 1:** John Collins/ESV - Subject is “him”, the victim of the attack. He* feels *like he’s going to be knocked over, despite the previous statement that he will\* “not be shaken.”**\*Option 2:** John MacArthur - Subject is “you”, the perpetuator of the attack. The perpetuator is on shaky ground. The previous statement of the victim being “not greatly shaken” reigns true.**Option 3:** Rachelle Harris - Subject is “attack.” The attack is like a wall or tottering fence. The attack applies pressure to the victim, but worse yet, attempts to undermine the foundation it is attached to. That foundation could be “the people of God” and this attack is “sowing discord among the people”. This attack could be spreading falsehoods about the man in the high place, the king.

In all situations it is clear that the attack is bad for the victim and it is conducted by wicked men.

> [!question] ❓
> How do we know that these enemies are bad?
> _1. Only intent is to thrust someone down (not justice) 2. Take pleasure in lies/falsehood 3. They curse_

The extent of their wickedness could be even worse. We don’t know the subject of the blessing, it could be a person, in which case they are talking out of both sides of their mouth. Worse still it could be God! These enemies could be blessing God yet cursing him internally.

Selah, David stops for a breath

## Trusts the Lord (v. 5-10)

David returns to the same statement in verses 1-2

> [!question] ❓
> Do you see any differences from verse 1-2 and verses 5-7?

- He now commands his soul to wait
- My hope
- My glory

I find it interesting that coming out of the section with confronting enemies, David commands his soul to wait in silence. Even now soul, do not complain, wait patiently on God.

David then moves his attention to those reading or listening. He has a command for us.

“Trust in him at all times”

> [!question] ❓
> What does he mean to “pour out your heart before him?”

“Pour out your heart before him.” - Empty the contents of your heart to the Lord. Share your anxiousness, your fear, your emotion with him.

David reminds us that God is safe. He is a refuge. He will shield you behind thick stone walls. He can handle your emotion and your enemies.

Selah. David takes a breath before he challenges our ideas about trust. He’s going to shares two ways that trust in God is better than something else in verses 9 and 10.

Let’s look at verse 9

“breath” - hevel, used in Ecclesiastes. A breath, vapor, vanity!

“delusion” - lie or falsehood

“low estate and high estate” - Poor people, rich people. People without clout and people with clout

“balances” - scales, like old fashioned ones

> [!question] ❓
> So what does verse 9 mean?

\*Think of scales used for measuring. You put something on the right side, and something on left side. You do this to see if they weigh the same.

David puts people of low and high rank on one side. They rise on the scale, they are light, not heavy. They are lighter than a breath. We would \***\*not\*\***value this. We value heavy things on the scale. \*

> [!question] ❓
> What’s on the other side of the scale?
> _God. He is of worth, he is heavy and has value. _

So we learn the first thing to not trust in, is position. It does not matter if we are low or high, or if someone is lower or higher in rank. It’s meaningless compared to the power of God. God is greater than an social standing. He will continue to be your refuge no matter your social standing.

The next thing to not trust in is money.

v. 10 tell us to not put trust in extortion. Threatening people for money, nor should we hope in money received in robbery. Even if you just get richer, honestly, don’t set your heart on it! Money cannot save you. You cannot purchase stone walls that are as thick as the Lord’s fortress. You will only find true refuge with in his stronghold.

## Praises the Lord (v. 11-12)

What’s David’s response to these two lessons? Praise to God. It makes sense! God is far greater than either of these things so let us praise him.

> [!question] ❓
> What does it mean when David writes, “Once God has spoken, twice have I heard this”

John Collins helps here, it is to indicate that the idea is sure. The best analogy I could come up with this is like when you have a conversation with three friends. Your first friend says something like, “the sky is blue.” You heard him, but you turn to friend to your other friend and ask, “what did he say?” and the other friend says, “he said the sky is blue.” You’ve heard him twice, though he has spoken once. This other source has verified it’s true

> [!question] ❓
> What has God spoken?

”That power belongs to God and that to you, O Lord, belongs steadfast love.”

Wow what a reassuring praise that David lifts up. He submits his life and power to God, “No God power belongs to you!”, but also teaches us this while saying it.

He ascribes steadfast love to God. Just as the rock cannot be shaken, neither can the Lord’s love. It does not crumble or falter. It is consistent and always present.

David ends this Psalm in a way that is reminscent to the Proverbs his son would later write. He looks back on all he has said and confidentially declares that God will render to a man, what his work entails. Summed up, the righteous will prosper and the wicked will fail. The righteous will stand within the walls of the fortress of God, alive and well. The wicked will fail with no success in their attacks.
