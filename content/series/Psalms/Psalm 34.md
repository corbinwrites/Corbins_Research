# **Questions to Ask**

<!-- Column 1 -->

1. **What is the structure of this psalm?
   **This is an acrostic! An alphabet poem. From a logical stand point we can break this into two parts 1. Praise 2. Preaching
2. **What kind of psalm is this?**
   Primarily, Thanksgiving with elements of wisdom
3. **If known, what is the historical context for this psalm?**
   **1 Samuel 21:10-15**, worth noting that this tie is not _vital_ to interpretation, but a nice to know
   MacArthur has a helpful comment that “Abimelech” like the word “Pharaoh” is a designation of kingship, rather than a proper name.
4. **Who are the subjects within the psalm?** 3. I - David 4. He - The LORD 5. His Saints, the righteous, his servants 6. Those who do evil, the wicked
5. **What is the intent of this psalm? How would it be used?
   **To praise the Lord for answered prayer and to share wisdom on how to receive the Lord’s blessing
6. **How does singing this in corporate worship shape the hearts of the covenant people?** 7. This reminds believers that the Lord provides. He is close to the afflicted and that he cares for his people.
   > \*“The logic is clear: by recounting the reasons for gratitude to God for his care, the members of God’s people encourage one another to still greater trust and faithfulness.”

- C. John Collins\*

# Lesson

**Main Idea: **Let us praise God! He provides, he is close to the afflicted and cares for those who fear him. By singing praise together, the members of God’s people encourage one another to still greater trust and faithfulness.

## Preamble

This Psalm contains elements of “wisdom” and I think it’s important for us to keep in mind that as with all points of Wisdom in the bible they are _universal_ (things that are always true). I’ll quote Duvall and Hayes’ Grasping God’s Word. Here they are talking about proverbs, but it’s very applicable here as well,

This is the same paradigm that David operates in this Psalm. As New Testament believers, it’s important for us to keep the breadth of scripture in mind. Just as Duvall and Hayes’ here talk about the basic approach of life as seen in Proverbs and in this Psalm, so there exists 3 other wisdom books that share exceptions to this rule (2 of which are relevant tonight):

> [!question] ❓
> Does anyone know what the books are?
> Does anyone want to try out what the exceptions are that we learn from these books?

7. The righteous can suffer - Job
8. A rational, ordered approach cannot provide the ultimate meaning of life - Ecclesiastes
9. Romantic love between a husband and wife is irrational - Song of Songs

If we keep in mind the truths evident in the book of Job and Ecclesiastes (the relevant exceptions to wisdom here), and you follow the wisdom set forth in verses 8-14, it’s possible you could find that:

10. You know God, you *are *righteous, and you *are *delivered from trouble (Proverbs approach)
11. You know God, you *are *righteous and *are not *delivered from trouble (Job approach)
12. You manage to follow the wisdom on your own strength and are blessed, but still lack the most important thing, a relationship with God, thus are unfulfilled in your life and eternal purpose (Ecclesiastes approach)

It’s important for us to realize that all 3 are possible for us as believers! Even Ecclesiastes! Even those who are mature in the faith can “muscle up, forge on” and try to do it without God. I know I have had moments in my life, like this last week, where I have seen myself do it.

## Praise (v. 1-10)

Like previous Psalms we have studied this first section functions almost as a “call to worship.”

### Let *Us *Praise the Lord (v. 1-3)

> [!question] ❓
> Reading verses 1-3 what is the feeling you get?
> _Joy_

This a joyful man who wants the people of God to join in with his joy.

Verse 3, David calls the people of God to magnify the Lord with him.

> [!question] ❓
> What does he mean to magnify?
> _Exalt, glorify, sing about the importance of God, praise enthusiastically_

David, in his invitation to corporately magnify the Lord, implicitly connects verse 1 and 2 to the manner in which we magnify! So while David, blesses the Lord at all times, so should the people of God do they same. This is the case for all the points he makes here.

Yes, David will be teaching us a bit later, but he’s already begun a little bit, by showing us *how *to worship.

13. He talks about this continual praise
14. His soul make its boast in the Lord. Here he is using personification for his soul.

> [!question] ❓
> What does this personification mean?
> _I will ascribe power to the Lord rather than my own ability. My soul could boast in itself but it will instead boast in the Lord_

15. Using synonymous parallelism, David expands slightly to share that this quality of the soul is _humility_. “Let the humble hear and be glad.”

### Let *Me *Praise the Lord (v.4-7)

After this corporate “call to worship”, David reflects specifically on how the Lord has saved him. It’s important to remember that this is a song sung by the congregation, and just as we discussed two weeks ago, the Hebrew believer, and us, can insert ourselves into this section as well.

> v. 4 “I sought the Lord and he answered me
> an delivered me from all my fears”

Here David reflects on the experience he had with the king of Gath. The Lord saw fit to bless David’s ruse. As David sings this in confidence, so can we reflect on those words exactly as they appear on the page. A question for you to consider on you own, **has the Lord answered a prayer of yours and delivered you from the fear in that situation? **If so, you might feel like David does. Joyous! What a great thing to celebrate.

> [!question] ❓
> What does David mean in verse 5? What does it mean for a face to be radiant?
> _Image of God’s Favor/Blessing. The “Shekinah” Glory
> Exodus 34:29-35_

David invokes Moses imagery with this phrase.

> **29 **When Moses came down from Mount Sinai, with the two tablets of the testimony in his hand as he came down from the mountain, Moses did not know that the skin of his face shone because he had been talking with God.[[a](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%2034%3A29-35&version=ESV#fen-ESV-2526a)] **30 **Aaron and all the people of Israel saw Moses, and behold, the skin of his face shone, and they were afraid to come near him. **31 **But Moses called to them, and Aaron and all the leaders of the congregation returned to him, and Moses talked with them. **32 **Afterward all the people of Israel came near, and he commanded them all that the Lord had spoken with him in Mount Sinai. **33 **And when Moses had finished speaking with them, he put a veil over his face.
[[Bible/ESV/Exodus/Exod 34#29|Exodus 34:29-35]]

As we look at Exodus this idea of “looking” to God is not simply a glance, but a deep examination, a gaze. Moses spends _lots_ of time with God listening to his Word and faithfully carried it to Israel. He spent so much time, his face literally shone! In contrast is the “face of shame.”

Collins helpfully recaps this section

> For the “face” to be “ashamed” is for someone to be disappointed at not finding what was hoped for. The faithful, though, will not find their prayers have been in vain.

The way verse 4 and 5 build on each other are fascinating. To paraphrase the truth we are personally singing here, **If I seek the Lord, he will answer me. If I look to him (gaze at him) I will not be disappointed with the answer he gives me. **

David defends his case further in **verses 6 and 7**

v.6, He praises the Lord for how He saved David from his trouble. Again, let’s think about singing these words as a congregation though. David describes himself as the poor man, but so do the people of Israel sing those same words, and can’t we as well. I am the poor man who cried, you are the poor man who cried. As New Testament believers and at a base level, the Lord has answered our prayers a differently than David and that has taken he form of Jesus Christ’s life, death, and resurrection.

We are all poor men and women in need of deliverance from the Lord.

David finishes this section with a mention to a very observable, specific way the Lord delivered Israel in the OT (Gen 16:7,18, 19, 31; Joshua 5). He provides a physical example beyond the person to illustrate how God has physically been present, protecting his people! The presence of the Angel of the Lord! Certainly a known, powerful image of God’s providence for the Hebrew believer.

### Let *Us *Fear the Lord (v. 8-10)

8-10 are interesting! It feels like a blend of praise and preaching, like the colors that get too close to one another on a watercolor, or when a DJ crossfades from one song to another. There are elements of _both_ praise and preaching in this small section.

Then he moves into verse 8…

> [!question] ❓
> What are we to “taste?”
> \*Finding refuge in the Lord (note the illustrative parallelism in 8a&b)

- So what does David mean by “taste and see that the Lord is good?”
  _David asks the believer to personally experience. It’s one thing to talk about the food, it’s another to try it yourself. You will try this and see that the lord is “good”, he is kind, loving, and seeks to protect you._

Those who experience this “delightful dish” will be blessed.

He expands to say that the Lord not only protects, but provides! The qualification is that we “fear” God.

> [!question] ❓
> What does it mean to “fear” God?
> _Submit, respect, obey, yes, perhaps just straight up fear, like how one would fear a thunderstorm in a plane_

David finishes this section of praise with a bit of wordplay. With all this talk of food he talks about how “young lions” are hungry. We are not like these predator/scavengers risking life and limb just to have a small morsel or a fresh kill. No, we have the Lord laying a feast before us. One where we are safe and filled with food.

## Preaching (v. 11-22)

And so we move away from the one color of the watercolor to the next color (or if you prefer from the last song). David gives a strong transition here: “I will teach you.” We have moved from praise to wisdom now!

### _How_ to Fear the Lord (v. 11-12)

And David asks a pretty good question here, “Do you want a long life that is good?” Yes, I think we all do. Well David has some specific instructions here:

> [!question] ❓
> How many pieces of wisdom do we see in verses 13 & 14?

16. Keep your tongue from evil
17. Don’t speak deceit
18. Turn away from evil
19. Turn toward good
20. Seek peace
21. Pursue peace

C. John Collins points out,

> “The dominant characteristic of the holy life described here is its social orientation; that is, the faithful are to keep from speaking evil and deceit, which hurts their neighbors. They are to turn away from (doing) evil (cf. Job 1:1; Ps. 37:27; Prov. 3:7; 14:16; 16:6) in order to do good, that is, to act generously and kindly—which is further amplified as “seek peace and pursue it.”
> Excerpt From
> ESV Expository Commentary: Psalms–Song of Solomon
> Iain M. Duguid
> This material may be protected by copyright.

Certainly this will translate into behavior that honors God, but it is interesting that David is interested in our treatment with one another.

### *Why *We Should Fear the Lord (v. 15-22)

One of the most frustrating subjects for me to learn in school was calculus. No matter what teacher I asked, they never had a good answer for “what I would do with it.” I later learned the purpose of this subject, but I think what would have made them an excellent teacher was if they had told me the _purpose_ in learning this subject.

Thankfully, David is a good teacher. In verses 15-22 he gives us reasons why we should fear (respect, be obedient to) the Lord. He equates righteousness with those that fear the Lord. So let’s list them!

> [!question] ❓
> **What does the Lord do for the righteous according to verses 15-22?**
> The Lord sees the righteous (v. 15)
> The Lord hears the cry of the righteous (v. 15, 17)
> The Lord delivers the righteous from trouble (v. 17, 19)
> The Lord is near the brokenhearted (humble) (v.18)
> The Lord keeps the bones of the righteous (v.20)
> The Lord redeems the life of the righteous (v.22)
> The Lord does not condemn the righteous (v.22)

**Brokenhearted and Crushed in Spirit- **refer to people who are humbled before God, the pride and stubbornness in their hearts being subdued (John C Collins)

In contrast to those that are righteous are those who are wicked. Now keep in mind that David has equated those who fear the Lord to those who are righteous. So who would be wicked? Those who don’t fear the Lord. Those who do no obey, surrender, humble themselves before the Lord.

> [!question] ❓
> **What does the Lord do to the wicked according to verses 15-22?
> **The Lord’s face (eyes and ears) are against the wicked (v.16)
> The Lord does not let anyone remember the wicked (v.16)
> The Lord afflicts the wicked (v. 21)
> The Lord slays the wicked (v. 21)
> The Lord condemns the wicked (v. 21)

There are many, many interesting contrasts in this passage. David heavily uses wordplay.

v.15 & 16 - The Lord has his face towards the righteous, but turned away from evil. His focus is only on those whom he loves

v. 19 & 21 - Affliction comes for both the wicked and righteous. However, the wicked are slain and the righteous are delivered.

v. 21 & 22 - The wicked are condemned and the righteous are not condemned

v. 18 & 20 - Those with crushed spirits (pride) will not have crushed bones.

What David is writing about is a righteous person. Someone who seeks to be obedient to God and live a life that fears God, like we read in verses 11-12. We should strive for that. However, we have been given an ultimate example of what a righteous life looks like, and that is the life of Jesus. Let’s flip over to [[Bible/ESV/John/John 19#36|John 19:36]]…

> [[Bible/ESV/John/John 19#28|John 19:28-37]]

_The Death of Jesus_
[28] After this, Jesus, knowing that all was now finished, said (to fulfill the Scripture), “I thirst.” [29] A jar full of sour wine stood there, so they put a sponge full of the sour wine on a hyssop branch and held it to his mouth. [30] When Jesus had received the sour wine, he said, “It is finished,” and he bowed his head and gave up his spirit.

_Jesus’ Side Is Pierced_
[31] Since it was the day of Preparation, and so that the bodies would not remain on the cross on the Sabbath (for that Sabbath was a high day), the Jews asked Pilate that their legs might be broken and that they might be taken away. [32] So the soldiers came and broke the legs of the first, and of the other who had been crucified with him. [33] But when they came to Jesus and saw that he was already dead, they did not break his legs. [34] But one of the soldiers pierced his side with a spear, and at once there came out blood and water. [35] He who saw it has borne witness—his testimony is true, and he knows that he is telling the truth—that you also may believe. **[36] For these things took place that the Scripture might be fulfilled\*: “Not one of his bones will be broken.” **[37] And again another Scripture says, “They will look on him whom they have pierced.”

John includes this reference to [[Bible/ESV/Psalms/Ps 34|Psalm 34]] because the Psalm shows what a righteous person looks like. Jesus is the full realization, (John says fulfilled) of righteousness. If you wish to go even further into righteousness than what David writes, mimic the life of Jesus.

What’s even more fascinating about this tie-in is that verse 22 is fully realized in this exact moment as well. In Jesus’ death, has made atonement, redeemed, the life of all his servants. The Hebrew believer looks forward to this point in time for their salvation (Romans 4:3), we look back to this point in time for our salvation (Romans 3:23-24). Both of us look to the Messiah for our redemption.

So let us not squander this costly prize that Jesus freely gives us. Let us fear God! We should be obedient to him, keep our tongue from evil, our feet pursuing peace, our face towards goodness, and our gaze constantly on the Lord.

\*_Note: This Psalm is not a prophecy, as it contains real exhortation for both Hebrew and New Testament believers. It does not foretell the coming of the Messiah. However, it is inspired! John recalls this Psalm while writing his account, and I’m quite certain that same Spirit was in David when he wrote the Psalm. John writes that this is fulfilled because Jesus perfectly exemplifies a righteous man. There is no greater example. _
