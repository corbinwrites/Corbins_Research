Jerusalem is on a hill and covered in large walls. While writing this lesson, I imagined the Psalmist standing on one of the walls, watching Pilgrims come in for one of the feasts. He looks to different areas around Jerusalem over the course of a few days to paint the picture.

The Psalmist first looks to the temple. As a Son of Korah, he had the privilege of serving there and he reflects on it with a smile. He writes from the perspective of one who does not have the privilege that he has. Someone who is traveling from a town, _not_ in Jerusalem.

## Outline

1. The Desire for the House of the Lord
2. The Drive for the House of the Lord
3. The Delight of the Lord

What kind of Psalm is this?

Psalm of Ascent! A pilgrimage!

## **The Desire for the House of the Lord**

Verse 1

The Psalmist immediately introduces the main focus of this Psalm, the Lord’s dwelling place.

> [!question] ❓
> _What is the dwelling place of the Lord in the Old Testament?_
> The temple!

interestingly the psalmist contrasts the comfort of a dwelling place with a powerful title! Lord of Hosts! Hosts is also translated as “armies” like we hear about the “Heavenly Hosts” in other parts of scripture.

The psalmist has a mind to to say the Lord’s dwelling belongs the General of the heavenly army! Why he draws this comparison we’ll examine in a few more verses…

Verse 2

but for now, the Psalmist next introduces **the desire for the house of the Lord**.

The psalmist *longs for *the Lord’s dwelling. Going so far as to say he faints for courts of the LORD (Yahweh’s Courts)

> [!question] ❓
> _In everyday life what causes someone to faint?_
> Lack of oxygen, lack of food, lack of water
>
> The psalmist says that the house of God is **essential like this**.

Here, in this special place, they psalmist says that his heart and flesh, (spirit and body) sing to joy to the Living God. This is the picture of a worshipper with outstretched hands and an outstretched heart. Leaving all cares of social judgement and personal worry behind. The Psalmist seeks only to sing for joy!

Verse 3

The Psalmist dives into seemingly takes a left turn and into figurative language

> Even the sparrow finds a home,
> and the swallow a nest for herself,
> where she may lay her young

At the end we find out *where *these nests are being built

> at your altars, O LORD of hosts,

So there are literal birds building literal nests by the altars in the temple

> [!question] ❓
> *Why would the Psalmist include this detail about birds and their nests?
> *A symbol of protection, safety, comfort

Notice **protection** and the Psalmist reiterates the title here**the Lord of Hosts**! The Psalmist here, tying the safety of the birds and the title of God is making a point that there is a feeling of comfort, safety, closeness in the house of the Lord.

Verse 4

He says

> blessed are those who dwell in your house!”

Almost as if the Psalmist says, The birds are to be envied for their special nests! But he does speak here of the Levites, or those who serve. They constantly have an opportunity to sing praise to God.

> [!question] ❓
> *What is the Psalmist’s desire for in the house of the Lord?
> *Closeness with God, Comfort, Protection

Transition

The Psalmist ends with a Selah to breakup the Psalm for our benefit. And so honoring the Selah break we look to our next section with fresh eyes! The Psalmist turns his eyes from the temple to those walking into Jerusalem for the feast. They are _ascending_ Jerusalem\*\*to the temple, walking through a valley.

The Psalmist, in the next 3 verses will talk about…

## **The Drive for the House of the Lord**

Drive here referring to:

- the force within propelling the pilgrims
- the journey to get to the city of Zion

Verse 5

The Psalmist next confers blessings to another group of people, those whose strength is in God and

> In whose heart are the highways to Zion

> [!question] ❓
> _What does he mean by, “In whose heart are the highways to Zion?”
> He’s probably referring to the Jewish believer who desires to be at Jerusalem to be with God! Probably to worship and celebrate God._

The Psalmist drops a little wisdom on the Hebrew believer here. Blessed are you who find **strength in God** and**those of you who desire to be with him**. He’s not wrong! Moses had certainly written this many centuries earlier

> You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your might.
> [[Bible/ESV/Deuteronomy/Deut 6#4|Deut 6:4]]

Verse 6

The Psalmist continues by talking about “they.”

> [!question] ❓
> _Who is they?_
> The pilgrims, “those whose strength is in you”

The Valley of Baca is a dry valley. Some translators said this could be a valley filled with Balsam trees, my bible dictionary says there are tombs there in the present, but the point is that it is certainly a dry valley both spiritually and physically.

However this dry valley is made into a place of springs!

> [!question] ❓
> _Why does the pslamist use the image of a spring?_
> Refreshment, Life!

J. Mac. says the pilgrims make it a valley filled with joy! Which I like. It speaks to the fact that this dusty dry valley is filled with so many pilgrims coming to worship the Lord for a festival. In a bit of creative irony, the psalmist says, “oh yes, the rain, the thing that normally makes pools, also does make this a place with springs too.”

Verse 7

The pilgrims go from strength to strength. They are not weary, they are filled with**joy and excitement** to worship the Lord.**This joy to be with the Lord is the drive** that puts them through a dusty valley.**This love for God spills out of their hearts** and fills the dusty valley with metaphorical springs of life

Verse 8

As we come into verse 8, note the selah break. So that would indicate that verse 8 would be tied to verses 5-7. The Psalmist prays that the Lord will hear the prayer of what _he just said_. In this final verse of the section the Psalmist artfully adds another layer to the pre-established statement of fact. Yes, those that have strength and desire for the Lord of are blessed.
The Psalmist say, but, Lord, I pray that you _will_ bless them as well! Make this come to pass. May the pilgrim coming to Zion be strong! May they all come with joy!
It seems as if the Psalmist makes the section a statement *and *a prayer. Very cool.

Transition

With the final Selah, the Psalmist, perched on top the walls of Jerusalem, looks back inside the walls of the city and to the temple. He sees the pilgrims streaming into the temple with joy and excitement to worship God. He joins in joy of the pilgrims and in the remaining verses writes about…

## **The Delight in the Lord**

Before we get into it though, I’m going to take a small digression on verse 9. It was a bit confusing to me and I wanted to share some insight that might be helpful. I have to give credit, where credit is due, John MacArthur’s commentary drew a few helpful connections from me that you’ll see in a bit here.

Verse 9

The Psalmist begins with a small prayer

> [!question] ❓
> *What does he mean by “our shield?”
> *Ruler can also be translated as shield

Flip to **Hosea 4:18**

You’re probably like that “Corbin, this is a weird verse. Why are you bringing this up?”

**Does anyone have a callout as to what that word rulers can also be translated as? \*\*\***Shields!\*\*\*

Yeah, so going back to the Psalm, we can re-read this as

This parallelism is verse 9 is an **illustrative** one. It says the same thing with an image, to reconvey the first idea. This image is backed by the use of the phrase, your anointed, which is used in reference to the Kings of Israel.

All this to say, the Psalmist says “please bless our King.”

At first glance it seems random! Don’t worry, if we look to the grammar, we can infer a reason.

Verse 10

Opens with “For” which is an indicator of an explanatory clause.

The Psalmist explains that a day in the temple courts is better than a thousand elsewhere!

The Psalmist wants the Lord to bless the King with a prosperous rule so that he can keep coming back to the temple. A smart civic minded pilgrim!

So whats the point?

**The Psalmist delights in the Lord so much he asks that that King is blessed so that he can participate in the feast with joy and/or so that the land and temple is safe so he can come and worship**.

With the layer of kingly blessing peeled back, let’s go one layer deeper and examine the content of verses 10-12 for what they are, **pure exuberant delight in the Lord**!

The Pilgrim is satisfied to be to be a lowly doorkeeper in the house rather than dwell in a tent of wickedness. He could simply relax, rest, get comfy, but he’s in a tent. He’s not in the strong impermeable house of the Lord. He’s outside the Lord’s house and nomadic. No he’d rather be working, in the strong house of his God.

The imagery interpretation aside, we still may ask the question, “Why would he take a lowly position?”

Verse 11

Anticipating our question the Psalmist opens with “For.” Another explanatory clause! Gives us 5 good reasons why.

> [!question] ❓
> *What are the reasons you see?
> *A sun, shield, favor, honor, does not withhold good things.

> [!question] ❓
> *Why does he describe God as a sun and shield?
> *I’ll be honest, I don’t know Hebrew enough to say it _doesn’t_ mean ruler as well. It has a similar root word, but has extra letters. So I’d hazard to say this does just mean shield.

> [!question] ❓
> _How has the Lord bestowed favor and honor?_

The Psalmist praises God, he _delights_ in him for the good gifts that He has given him.

Verse 12

The Psalmist concludes with a confident statement, those who trust in God are blessed!

## Application Questions

4. The Psalmist finds comfort, protection, and joy in the temple of the Lord, but Jesus tore the curtain of the temple making way for us to enter the Holy of Holies anywhere on Earth (Hebrews 10:19-22). Just as the Psalmist finds these attributes in the temple, do we feel that same way about our relationship with Jesus right now?
5. As a New Testament believer we no longer make pilgrimages for feasts, but we do take a longer journey, one to Heaven. Paul describes the christian journey as a race (1 Cor 9:24, [[Bible/ESV/2 Timothy/2 Tim 4#7|2 Tim 4:7]]), a bit of a faster journey with a final destination. Interpreting our pilgrimage more symbolically, how has the journey been for you?
   1. Are you walking with joy to reach our Heavenly Zion?
   2. Are you walking from strength to strength or are you weak?
