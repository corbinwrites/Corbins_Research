# Big Group Question

1. In verse 14, James asks if it is good to not have works. What are works? Are there any passages inside and outside of James that help give examples?

2. In verse 18, James says he will show his faith by his works. What does he mean here? How can he do that?

# Teaching Notes

## Outline

## Intro

Imagine you have a friend who is a vegetarian. You know this because they walk up to you and say “I’m a vegetarian.” You say, “cool.” You both go to dinner and your friend then orders a double western cheeseburger with bacon. “Woah. I thought you said you were a vegetarian?”
They respond, “I am!” then taking a giant bite out of the burger. “Really?” you ask. “Yes. I fully believe I am a vegetarian!” another bite. Clearly this person’s actions do not match what they believe about themselves. For one to consider themself a vegetarian it requires you to match the diet fully. You must strive to eat no meat. That is what is required to consider yourself a vegetarian.

This is what James is talking about here in this passage tonight. We can, and should, claim belief, but we should back it up with action. To _proclaim_ we are a Christian is the first step, the next is to _act_ like a Christian.

## Question (v.14)

Where many sections previously have started with a command, James instead starts with a question. This question, as we’ll see, certainly has an underlying command!

There are two key words used in this question “faith” and “works.” Faith, as we know, is the professed belief in the Lord Jesus Christ. It is the belief that he is fully man, fully God and the third member of the Trinity. He died an atoning death on a cross to cover our sins. Then he rose from the grave three days later to demonstrate his power over life and death. It is through Jesus that we are granted eternal life. This is the faith that James has in mind here.

Looking at this passage there is a critical phrase, “if someone says.” You’ll see the character of “someone” mentioned twice in the passage. “Someone” is an object used to build James’ argument. Did he have a certain person in mind? We’re not sure. At the very least though, James is standing up an opponent to build his persuasive argument around. James has allowed the role of someone to be played by anyone. He doesn’t insert a qualifier for someone (not rich, poor, christian, non-christian), this mask of “someone” can be put on by any of us. So a good question to ask as we examine the passage is “am I ‘someone’?” or said differently, am I the person James would be trying to persuade?

Note that someone *says *he has faith. Given the question following James initial question, “Can that faith save him?” It is implied in the writing that the person claiming to have faith, but not may not have faith at all.

If this person claims to love Jesus but does not show it? Does that faith save him?

## Case Study (v. 15-17)

James gives us an extreme example of a work here. It is extreme not in that it is an extreme work of love, but that it is extremely callous of “someone.”

Let me point a few words and then I’ll set the scene for us.

> [!question] ❓
> **What are some words and phrases that describe the people in this scene in verse 15?**
> “Brother or sister” shows that James is talking about a fellow christian
>
> “Poorly clothed” Douglas Moo points out that this is the greek _gymnos_ where we get *gymnasium *from. It means naked. These are not just dirty clothes, they have gaping holes in them.
>
> “Lacking daily food” in the same way that you can visually see their clothing, you can visually see this christian is lacking in food. They are very hungry.

So here’s the scene:

I walk into mission college and I find Jason with burnt clothing, his face is gaunt. I ask around and find out that his house has burnt down and those are his only remaining clothes. He lost his wallet and phone in the fire so he has no way to access his bank account and he hasn’t eaten in two weeks. Well… I need to do something for Jason.

So I go up to him after service and I do something huge for him, I grab hold of him and say, “Good bye! Stay warm! Have a nice meal!” Then release him.

Wow. What an act of love right? Surely Jason’s problem has been fixed by my nice words! Wrong.

Have I done any work to warm my brother? Have done anything to fill my brother with food? No!

James asks the right question. “What good is that?” Said another way, “how does that help Jason?” The answer is, it doesn’t. He has real felt needs, he has someone he needs to meet them given his desperate situation.

John Macarthur points out that James again has an eye to [[Bible/ESV/Matthew/Matt 25#31|Matthew 25:31-46]]. We’ve come back to this passage many times, but Jesus explains that what separates the sheep from the goats is their willingness to serve those in need. Here, in this case study, the one in need is not served.

To that end, the person saying, “Go in peace” would be found to be a goat, someone who is cast away from the sight of Jesus. Very directly, this person did not “feed those who were hungry” thus he/she would have a dead faith. Which is what James correctly asserts in verse 17.

This is an uncomfortable truth! If we don’t righteously behave according to God’s revealed Word we have a dead faith. James anticipates his readers may have objections, but he does not back down from their protests. Instead he…

## Correction (v. 18-20)

This section is a _diatribe_ where the main speaker is addressing an imaginary objector, in order to make a point. The point, James reiterates again, is that faith and works are necessary. Commentator Robert Plummer imagines this section in fencing terms and I rather enjoyed the analogy. James switches out the boxing gloves for a fencing foil and is ready to duel with his objector. You could choose to think of these in terms of Judo, Chess, Scrabble or other competitive two-player sports.

### Initial Attack (v. 18a)

We see the objection is delivered by “someone.” “Someone” is on the attack with an assertion! (Remember that we can be “someone”)

“You have faith and I have works”

The objector creates a false dichotomy faith and works. Imagining as different classes, or different gifts. You have the gift of service and I have the gift of faith.

### Parry (v. 18b)

_Block_

“Show me your faith apart from your works”

James, parries by casting doubt on the quality of this objectors faith. This question is very similar to what is asked in verse 14?

Again, James has in mind Matthew. What will the objector have to show for faith at the final judgement?

He continues the parry with the claim “I will show you my faith by my works”

> [!question] ❓
> **In verse 18, James says he will show his faith by his works. What does he mean here? How can he do that?**
> He will live out his life in accordance with the word of God

### Riposte (v. 19)

_Answer_

James comes back with a retort. He references the Jewish _shema_ which is [[Bible/ESV/Deuteronomy/Deut 6#4|Deuteronomy 6:4]],

[4] “Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one.

James affirms it is good that the objector knows this basic truth. However, the objector may not like the company he finds he has…

“Even the demons know - and shudder”

### Attack (v. 20)

_New Attack_

James presses again with a new attack and brings us back to the first question. He calls the objector “foolish” because that’s exactly what this line of thinking is. He is not being rude by truthful. Foolishness is a “lack of good sense or judgement.” If the objector was to live their life in “faith” not doing anything the scripture says (for others, to God, for their own development) but simply just says, “I believe God.” This person will find themself in the same faith as demons. They will be shown that this faith apart from a life demonstrating is useless and that they will not pass the final judgement.

## Outro

So let’s not let our faith be simply lip-service! Let it be shone in the way that we actively conform our lives to God’s Word. We must press constantly into the mold of Christ. We do so not in our strength but through prayer and a desire for God.
