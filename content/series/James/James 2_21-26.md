## Abraham’s Works

> [!question] ❓
> **How is Abraham justified? Where do you see that?**
> James helpful recaps for us in verse 24, justified in works and not faith “alone.”
>
> **Is James saying that I can earn my justification through work? What’s the significance of “faith alone?”**
> When using the phrase faith alone, James is describing that dead faith we talked about last week in verse 17, or that faith that has emotion and knowledge but is still dead in verses 19-20. That faith is not enough. For us to be justified is to mean that our faith is displaying Christ through works.

James points to the culminating point of Abraham’s faith by recalling the story of placing Isaac on the altar. This is _not_ the point in which Abraham was justified, Abraham is justified when he accepts and believes in God. Rather, this is the climax of justification. We can see this in how James quotes [[Bible/ESV/Genesis/Gen 15#6|Genesis 15:6]] in verse 23, not as to imply that the Covenant of God was a prophecy, but that the culmination of that phrase occurs with the sacrifice of Isaac.

James describes how Abraham does the “work” of faith in three points: His active faith, completing faith, and fulfilling faith.

The “works” James is focusing on for Abraham here is offering his son Isaac. This argument he uses could be applied for various other faithful believers in other situations

### Active Faith (v.22)

“You see that faith was _active_ along with his works”

This word active suggests that faith was continually being lived out. Doug Moo describes this faith as ”a constant companion.” This is true when we look at the life of Abraham. He frequently talks with God on many things (covenant, intercession for Sodom, the Birth of Isaac). It is evident that he has faith in God, though he doesn’t always follow perfectly.

### Completing Faith

“and faith that was *completed *in his works”

But really the faith of Abraham is completed because it is _shown_. Plummer writes,

This account of putting Isaac on the altar shows how Abraham believed God _fully_ and _unwaveringly_. Now is the time where faith moves from the heart to the hand, feet, and mind for action.

> [!question] ❓
> **Open up to [[Bible/ESV/Genesis/Gen 22#1|Genesis 22:1-19]]. How does Abraham display his faith in a way we can see?**

> [[Bible/ESV/Genesis/Gen 22#5|Genesis 22:5]]
> [5] Then Abraham said to his young men, “Stay here with the donkey; I and the boy will go over there and worship and**come again to you**.”

Here is belief at work. Abraham knows that God has commanded him to sacrifice his son, yet he also remembers when God in [[Bible/ESV/Genesis/Gen 15|Genesis 15]] says that he would “be his shield” and that he would grant him “a people as numerous as the stars.” Now in [[Bible/ESV/Genesis/Gen 22|Genesis 22]], God asks Abraham to sacrifice his only son.

> [[Bible/ESV/Genesis/Gen 22#7|Genesis 22:7-8]]
> [7] And Isaac said to his father Abraham, “My father!” And he said, “Here I am, my son.” He said, “Behold, the fire and the wood, but where is the lamb for a burnt offering?” [8] Abraham said, “**God will provide for himself the lamb** for a burnt offering, my son.” So they went both of them together.

L. L. Morris describes faith as, “Abandoning all trust in one’s own resources… casting oneself unreservedly on the mercy of God… Faith implies complete reliance on God and full obedience to God.”

Here in this part, Abraham knows it is only the Lord who can provide for him. He rests fully in his faith. He does not lie to comfort Isaac, but he instead trusts in the covenant of the Lord. The Lord does not revoke his covenants and he trusts in that. What great believing faith displayed by Abraham! Here is a faith completed. When put to the test it holds true and is displayed through works.

Due to showing his active, completing faith, James shares Abraham has a…

### Fulfilling Faith

v.23 - “and the scripture was _fulfilled_…”

**Abraham is called righteous**- he is upright, not crooked. He is following the Word of the Lord. He is acting in a right way.** Abraham is a friend of God -**This is in reference to 2 Chronicles 20:7 and [[Bible/ESV/Isaiah/Isa 41#8|Isaiah 41:8]]

> 2 Chronicles 20:7
> _[7] Did you not, our God, drive out the inhabitants of this land before your people Israel, and give it forever to the descendants of Abraham your friend?_

> [[Bible/ESV/Isaiah/Isa 41#8|Isaiah 41:8]]
> _[8] But you, Israel, my servant,
> Jacob, whom I have chosen,
> the offspring of Abraham, my friend;_

How dear a title that Abraham is called, a “friend of God.” The creator God, Lord of the universe, orchestrator of all things, considers Abraham a friend. Someone he loves and treasures time with. How does Abraham get this title? By putting action to faith!

James recaps this in verse 24!

> [[Bible/ESV/James/Jas 2#24|James 2:24]]
> _You see that a person is justified by work and not by faith _<u>_alone_</u>

In other words, “Look! Abraham was justified by work and not only by faith.”

The key word, faith _alone_. Remember that James has some thoughts on the “faith” that doesn’t have works. He would say that’s not really faith at all and he’s right! We must do what th eLord commands.

## Rahab’s Works

James next moves on to a more unlikely example. Where many with a Jewish background would have high regard for Abraham, the father of faith (v. 21), Rahab may not be necessarily mentioned in the same breath. To mention Rahab to a Jewish audience wouldn’t leave the audience wondering which Rahab (there’s only one in the old testament), so why does James use the title of “prostitute” to help us identify? It could possibly be a look at how the most popular figure of the faith and perhaps more lowly regarded figure both have justifying works that are “in the same way!”

> [!question] ❓
> **Why did Rahab need to do something? What if she simply believed without action?**
> The spies would have been caught! Joshua would not have received words from the spy.

> [!question] ❓
> **Read Joshua 2:1–24. How does Rahab display her faith in a way we can see?** 4. She protects God’s people 5. She honors and submits to the Lord (v.8,11)
>
> She doesn’t do it perfectly. She lies, but look how new this faith is for her, look at how God rewards her righteousness by grafting her into the line of David and Jesus the Messiah! She is the mother of Boaz from Ruth.

I can’t find the source, but a commentator I read had a great tie up for this point of faith. While the title of prostitute remained, the sins of it did not as she was a believer in God and was adopted into his family. I think many of us can relate to Rahab’s story and the stain of sin. It makes sense that she is mentioned in both Hebrews and in James.

## Conclusion

James concludes our section by going back to the beginning, “faith apart from works is dead.” He uses another illustration to make his point. Faith is like our body and works is like our spirit. If you have no spirit, you have no life. You’re dead. No spirit means you’re a corpse.

So as we leave this section in James, let’s not be corpses in our faith! Let’s do the works of faith: living out God’s word each and every day!

## Small Notes

Synthesizing Romans 4 with [[Bible/ESV/James/Jas 2#14|James 2:14-24]] is a fun exercise. The lynchpin between the two is “belief.” Both James and Paul talk about how this essential and what we discover between the two is full definition of belief. Belief is not simply having a simple, head knowledge hope in the things of the bible, but practically living it out each and everyday. Nor is belief simply working to earn a way to heaven, but a genuine, transformative hope that changes our hearts into loving God with all that we do.
