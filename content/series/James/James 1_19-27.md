## Intro

Let me take you back to 2018. The Dodgers have an injured shortstop on their team and they need someone to fill that spot asap. The team finds an amazing replacement. A 4 time All-Star, two time Golden Glove, AND Platinum Glove. This guy knows baseball. The Dodgers trade to acquire his $16 million dollar contract. He has some ups and downs, but really he shows his true colors in the National League Championship Series. In Game 2 the Los Angeles Dodgers are playing against the Milwaukee Brewers. It’s the top of the fourth inning, neither team has scored. This player steps up to the plate and hits a grounder that goes perfectly between second and third base and into the hole. For most baseball players, that is a sure single. The camera pans to the Brewers shortstop, he recovers quickly, grabs the ball, and visually slows down to *casually throw the ball *to first.
Why would he throw slow and take his time?

The camera pans and we see the baseball player that was being paid $16 million dollars to play baseball slowly jogging, an almost walk, up the first baseline. The ball is thrown and the Dodgers player is firmly out.

This player’s name is Manny Machado. When interviewed by The Athletic, Manny says, “Obviously… I'm not the type of player that's going to be 'Johnny Hustle,' and run down the line and slide to first base and … you know, whatever can happen… That's just not my personality, that's not my cup of tea, that's not who I am.”

Needless to say at the end of the season the Dodgers did not seek to renew his contract and he is hated by Dodgers fans to this day.

It’s evident that the Dodgers and the fans _want a player that will hustle_! That will *do *something. It is not enough to know baseball, or to have a lot of awards, and simply be on the baseball diamond. Instead in all of life, but especially when it counts, we need to put forth effort. Baseball and faith, both require _hustle._ We must use our _use_ our knowledge and do something with it. All this to say, this section in James is about *doing,*it’s about spiritual sweat from our spiritual knowledge. We should be known for our action.

There’s three things for us to do here:

- Restrain Wrath
- Receive the Word
- Do the Word

## Restrain Wrath (v.19-20)

v.19

<u>Beloved brothers</u>: James is expressing his love for the readers of the letter (then _and_ now). He wants us to know, he is in it with us.

> [!question] ❓
> In verses 19 & 20, James writes that we are to be slow to anger because the anger of man does not produce the righteousness of God, the key message of these two verses. **Why then should we be quick to hear and slow to speak? What’s the connection?**
>
> There are two connections drawn by commentators: this is generally applicable advice in all realms of life, this specifically refers to our interactions with the Word of Truth

**General Advice****Plummer and Moo** take the position that this is general advice; they are correct. Those who are quick to hear, slow to speak, and slow to anger are sure to prevent anger and, inverse to the verse, produce the righteousness of God.

<u>Quick to hear</u>: when someone is talking you’re focused on what they’re saying. You’re listening intently and seeking to discover the meaning of what they’re saying. This could be asking questions along the way to challenge your assumptions, but you’re certainly _leading_ with a listening ear.
<u>Slow to speak: </u>If your ears are busy, your mouth shouldn’t be. The goal here is to listen twice as long as you’re talking. In moments of disagreement it can be tempting to speak quickly, but instead we should hear out the person in their entirety before we give a response.

<u>Slow to anger:</u> The best way to avoid anger is to simply be slow to anger. When in disagreement do not be hasty to be angry!

You’ll note the examples I just mentioned feature person to person interaction in a disagreement, however, there’s nothing limiting it to only that. This advice would be applicable when encountering new information, meeting someone for the first time, whatever you encounter in life.

**Approach to the Word of Truth** However this broad command can be narrowed. Encapsulated in Plummer and Moo’s position would be**Wiersbe and MacArthur’s** position. James mentions the word of truth in the previous verse and they would argue that extends to this verse as well.

<u>Quick to hear</u>: Are you quick to “hear” the word of truth when you read it? Are your defenses down? Are you actively working to interpret the meaning of the text and understand it’s meaning in your life?

<u>Slow to speak:</u> We should be slow to speak the word in that we should allow for it to have weightiness, holiness. Admittedly, this is not a strong area for me. Additionally this could be applied to flippantly presenting a bible verse in a conversation.
”I’m struggling with this trial I’m in.”

“Well, [[Bible/ESV/Philippians/Phil 4#13|Philippians 4:13]], “I can do all things through him who strengthens me.”
Maybe it would be best to be slow to speak the bible until you’ve heard your friend’s trial and definitively figured out if exhortation, encouragement, or rebuke is the best path.

<u>Slow to anger:</u> Let us not be angry when we work with the Word of Truth. I mentioned at the beginning of this James study that some of these commands could get a rise out of us and this is exactly that. When we feel the sting of conviction it would be wise for us to not be angry.

Both positions are wonderful applications for this bible verse. No matter what application you choose the the result of anger is clear. Anger, from man, does not produce righteousness.

> [!question] ❓
> Is there an anger that _can_ produce righteousness?

[[Bible/ESV/Ephesians/Eph 4#26|Ephesians 4:26]] indicates that yes, there is. It is possible to have an anger _not_ of man that does produce righteousness. This would be an anger that is _against_ sin. This would be anger directed toward _the concepts of_ injustice, immorality, ungodliness. However, even this anger can become bitter and become rooted in our ideas, Paul writes we should not let the day pass by and remain in anger- Be angry and do not sin; do not let the sun go down on your anger

> In other words, man’s anger (i.e., the anger of a fallen human) is almost always tainted with sin (but cf. Eph. 4:26). Such anger does not manifest itself in obedient behavior reflecting God’s righteousness. - Plummer

James, pivots and expands, he tells us not only is anger of man filthiness and rampant wickedness, but tells us to put away _all_ filthiness and rampant wickedness, not just sinful anger.

My sharp-eyed readers may notice that this terminology is similar language to the Ephesians (4:22) and Colossians (3:9) “put off” and “put on” and you’d be right! This “put away” is the same verb used in both references. This idea of taking off old, muddy rags. Get those old clothes off you and into the nearest fire. ## Receive the Word (v.21)

Instead, we are to receive the implanted, some translations use engrafted, word.

> [!question] ❓
> \*\*Why use horticultural terminology to describe, “the Word?”

As a word picture\*\*

> Just as a seed takes root and the roots spread throughout the soul so has the word of God done that in our heart

[5] Other seed fell on rocky ground, where it did not have much soil, and immediately it sprang up, since it had no depth of soil. [6] And when the sun rose, it was scorched, and since it had no root, it withered away. [7] Other seed fell among thorns, and the thorns grew up and choked it, and it yielded no grain. [8] And other seeds fell into good soil and produced grain, growing up and increasing and yielding thirtyfold and sixtyfold and a hundredfold.” [9] And he said, “He who has ears to hear, let him hear.”

Given the possible connection with [[Bible/ESV/Mark/Mark 4|Mark 4]] it is of great importance that we “care” for our seed. We make sure the soil is not rocky, it is well watered, and the weeds are pulled. Those various metaphoric phrases are contained in a single word here,*“meekness.” *For us to care for the seed of the implanted word we must be ready to receive it with meekness.

> [!question] ❓
> **What does it mean to be meek? How does it look to receive the word meekly?**
> Meek: Power under restraint. “A big man with soft hands” as Chris said.
>
> Surprisingly, this indicates that we do have power. The power to receive the word or to not receive it. We should restrain this power to not receive the word.
>
> We should accept it. Let it rest in our heart. We should not try to argue with it, nor twist it or conform it into our own thinking.

The consequences for us receiving the word are dire. James writes that it is _able to save our souls_. It’s important to note, that we’re not talking about initial salvation. We’re talking about judgement.

[[Bible/ESV/Matthew/Matt 7#21|Matthew 7:21-23]] says,

We could easily find ourselves a “worker of lawlessness” if we do not receive God’s word with meekness.

How can we tell if we are passive, nominal in our faith and headed for this terrible outcome? How can we tell if we are vibrant and passionate in our faith?

Thankfully for us James tells us in the next verse and gives us a choice in the remainder of the passage. The result of our actions can be worthlessness, or worthiness. Let’s first look how our actions can be worthless starting in verse 22.

## Be a Doer, Not A Hearer

v. 22 - James talks about we *should be *doers of the word, someone who takes action! No Manny Machados here! Not simply a hearer. In fact hearers are _deceiving_ themselves.

> [!question] ❓
> **How is the hearer deceived? What does he or she believe that is false?**
> Most likely the hearer is deceived about his or her salvation or even standing with the Lord. If he or she simply hears the gospel, but don’t do anything with it, then there would not be salvation for this person.

James solidifies how the hearer operates by making a comparison to a person that looks in a mirror and forgets what they look like.

> [!question] ❓
> **Why is someone who hears the word like someone who forgets what they look like?
> **No one who looks at a mirror remembers the placement of every single hair on their head, the exact curve of their nose, and how many eyelashes they have. If you wanted to fix your hair, you would have to look back into a mirror. Similarly is the person who simply hears the word of God. They’ll quickly forget and continue to go back again and again to the same passage. How can they remember?

This is in contrast to the doer!

v.25 - James describes the doer as the one who looks into the perfect law. This is the OT, but like we said last week, also the “Word of Truth.” All that has been talked about in the NT as well

> [!question] ❓
> **In verse 25, the one who looks into the perfect law must also persevere. What must this person persevere in? What does this person receive?**
> Doug Moo takes the position that this person perseveres in looking at the perfect law. No matter how difficult the word is, no matter if it makes you feel angry, this person continues to _consistently_ look at the law. This doer does not have his gaze waver. We are told that this person is blessed! A recipient of a gift from God.

> [!question] ❓
> **How would someone be blessed from looking and doing at the perfect law? Said another way How would it benefit someone to read and do the Bible?**
> They could receive wisdom into a trial.

The result for the hearer?

Grim! Look to result of these hearers in verse 26, their religion is worthless. James here in verse 26 gives us a **practical example** of <u>how hearing the word is not enough,</u> but it requires _action_.

The word of God is clear, James has given explicit command in verse 19 to bridle, to “reign in”, the animal that is the tongue. Not only here, but also proverbs speaks frequently of the tongue and the need to reign it in:

- [[Bible/ESV/Proverbs/Prov 15#2|Proverbs 15:2]], The tongue of the wise commends knowledge,
  but the mouths of fools pour out folly.
- [[Bible/ESV/Proverbs/Prov 25#23|Proverbs 25:23]], The north wind brings forth rain,
  and a backbiting tongue, angry looks.

If a professed believer simply hears these command, but does not do so, they have deceived their heart into thinking they are religious. “Religious” here means the act of worship and service. To put it another way, this person is ignoring the Word of God and saying whatever words they want. Responding in anger, crude talk, tearing people down, etc. This person believes they are acting in a holy way! They are truly deceived. James calls this religion of hearing and not doing, worthless, a false religion. Dirt is of greater value than this “religion” because you can at least fill a hole with it. This is empty, unrestrained actions.

Again, James contrasts this hearer with what a true believer _does_. He contrasts a worthless religion with _pure_ religion. Worship and service that is untainted with sin.

As a final note on being a doer James leaves with two more ways we can be doers:

- He encourages us to care for the weakest in their need
- To remain pure from the world, unstained

This is, of course, not all that is encompassed in a Christian life. There are far more things for Christians to do that would exemplify pure and undefiled religion.
