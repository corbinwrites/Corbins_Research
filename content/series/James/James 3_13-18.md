# Big Group Questions

1. In verse 15, James writes, “This is not the wisdom that comes down from above.” What is “this” referring to? What makes it demonic?
2. In verse 17, James describes wisdom first as “pure.” What does it mean for something to be “pure?”

# Teaching Notes

## Outline

## Intro

Tonight we’ll be talking a lot about wisdom. So we should probably define it.

> [!question] ❓
> **What is wisdom?**
> A wonderful example that I have used for many years is the difference between intelligence and wisdom. Intelligence is knowing that a tomato is a fruit, not a vegetable. Wisdom is knowing to not include a tomato in your fruit salad. Some might be tempted to call wisdom “knowledge practically applied”, but a better definition might simply be discernment. I would seek to discern what the best course of action is. A few examples of some questions where wisdom would be applied:
>
> - Which of the fruits in my cupboard _do_ go in a fruit salad?
> - What is the right way to ask this question to my friend?
> - How should I react to the man who cut in line?
> - How should I respond to the barista who just gave me a free coffee?
>
> Wisdom is the pursuit of what is the “best” course of action. As Christians, we know that naturally what is best will be aligned to what God has said is best.

## Context

We are coming right out of the previous section on controlling our speech. To not let it get away from us James finishes this section with an implicit command for us to be consistent with the character that Christ has put within us. We are grapevines that should product grapes!

## The Challenge for Wisdom (v. 13)

From that section, James naturally takes it a step further into an explicit challenge. He calls for those who are wise and understanding. He calls these believers to demonstrate good conduct that allow their works to blossom.

An interesting call back to chapter 2! We know that works are the living out of scripture, the commitment to live it out. These works are saving faith.

However, James qualifies this with final phrase, “in the meekness of wisdom”

The works should be displayed in such a way that his humble and wise. Those who display their good works will be humble.

We can interpret this passage as saying, “The wise and understanding conduct themselves well and show their works in a humble way.”

## Wisdom from Below (v. 14 - 16)

James signals a switch from these wise believers to those who are not with a single word, “but.”
He cautions these unwise, non-understanding believers against boasting and being false in the truth.

Plummer breaks down this verse very helpfully for us:

> _There are two implicit verses in this section. If we make these phrases explicit, the text would read: “But if [you claim to be wise while at the same time] you have bitter jealousy and selfish ambition in your hearts, do not boast [about your so-called wisdom] and be false to the truth.”_

How do you know if you are one of these? He has two identifiers: if you have bitter jealousy and selfish ambition.

> [!question] ❓
> **Is the qualifier “bitter” and “selfish” important? Is all jealousy bad? Is all ambition bad?**> Douglas Moo points out that the descriptors here** are very important and helpful**. The word for jealousy in the greek can also be rendered as “zeal.” As we know** zeal and jealousy** can be a very good, very powerful thing!
>
> - Jehu’s killing of the enemies of the Lord, 2 Kings 10:16, “Come with me and see my zeal for the LORD.”
> - David’s Zeal in the Psalms, [[Bible/ESV/Psalms/Ps 69#9|Psalm 69:9]], “For zeal for your house has consumed me and the reproaches of those who reproach you have fallen on me.”
>
> Additionally, “selfish” **ambition** is helpful here as well. Ambition can be good!
>
> - Paul’s ambition is to preach the gospel in new places! (Romans 15:20)
> - The aspiration of the office of overseer is a noble task! (1 Tim 3:1)

> [!question] ❓
> What does a **bitter jealousy** look like?
> Zeal can be a very _bad_ thing, when it is bitter.
>
> - Job 5:2, “Surely vexation kills the fool, and jealousy slays the simple.” Though Eliphaz says it, it is true
> - [[Bible/ESV/Proverbs/Prov 6#34|Proverbs 6:34]], “For jealousy makes a man furious, and he will not spare when he takes revenge.”

It’s a **selfish jealousy** that does not consider the things of the Lord. It can look like envy, where I desire something that was not mine to begin with. Something as simple as my neighbor has a new car, or wanting another job. It can be more complex where we desire something to just be ours without putting in the work, like having an envious desire of someone’s faith, without putting in the work into our own.

It can also look like **sinful jealousy**, where something was mine but was given to someone else. For example, being promised for a promotion or a position but it has been given to someone else. Now, it is certainly unjust, and we can weep about how that is evidence of fall. However, in that example we must examine if this bitter jealousy is only focused on the fact that *I *have been wronged.

Additionally the **“selfish” ambition** is also bad. Ambition for godly pursuits is a good thing, but one that seeks to propel ourselves for our own gain “selfish” is not.

Paul gives a helpful, technical definition, in Philippians.

> [[Bible/ESV/Philippians/Phil 2#3|Philippians 2:3]]
> [3] Do nothing from selfish ambition or conceit, but in humility count others more significant than yourselves.

Here you can see selfish ambition described as the _opposite_ of humility and it means that you’re looking _only_ to your own self-interests, *not *to the interest of others.

Selfish ambition seeks to acquire at any cost. Despite damages to others or _attempts_ to damage God, this type of ambition will seek to acquire it.

> [!question] ❓
> Practically, what does **selfish ambition** look like? 3. Tearing down others present a favorable outcome 1. Speaking down about another brother or sister’s gifts to make yourself appear more competent for a way to serve. “No they’re just being critical and striving for excellence.” 4. Only focused on saving, but not being generous with our finances. 2. “I’m saving for a house, I can’t do that.” 5. Chasing a friendship or relationship that will benefit you rather than desiring genuinely getting to know someone, or neglecting the current friendships you have.
>
> Anytime we seek to put people, or _God_, aside to achieve our purpose.

Neither bitter jealousy nor selfish ambition come from heaven. James describes it not only as earthly, and unspiritual, but as **demonic**. This “wisdom” is hellish!

James calls this way of thinking demonic because it can be a root problem! From this root of jealousy and selfish ambition he says that there can be “disorder” and “every vile practice” (also translated as “evil” in the LSB).

> [!question] ❓
> **Why does James say that jealousy and selfish is an indicator of disorder and vile/evil practice?**
> Think about the examples we shared earlier. They throw people aside. It certainly creates disorder to have someone at any opportunity seek to elevate themselves. That person is unreliable and a moment’s notice they could seek to elevate themself throwing any plan into disarray.
>
> Not only that but this person could be sinning to achieve their purpose, in an extreme sense.
>
> In a more realistic sense, this person could be harboring sin. For example anger, or dissatisfaction with God because their desires are unmet.

Thankfully, there is another kind of wisdom other than this hellish wisdom. There is…

## Wisdom from Above (v. 17 & 18)

James sharply contrasts this sinful wisdom with wisdom from Heaven! First he describes this wisdom as pure.

> [!question] ❓
> **What does James mean when he says pure?**
> Without sin, Without fault!
> Callback: [[Bible/ESV/James/Jas 1#27|James 1:27]]
>
> [27] Religion that is pure and undefiled before God the Father is this: to visit orphans and widows in their affliction, and to keep oneself unstained from the world.
>
> It acts in the interests of others, it is uninfluenced by the evils of worldly thinking.

But purity is only where it starts! In same way that bitter jealousy and selfish ambition are the root for the fruit of disorder and vile practices. So is the purity of this wisdom the root that leads to these fruits listed.

Peaceable: Brings or gives peace. Doug moo points out that for their to be peace the next qualities are paramount.

Gentle: Considerate, Idea of being caring for others. Think of when you have a child hold a small rabbit, you say, “gentle!” It is an action that must be done _to_ someone or something else.

Open to Reason: Submissive. Additionally, for one to have this quality, there must be “reason” to interact with. If someone says, “I’m open to reason.” and then shut their eyes and closed their ears, they would be intentionally shutting themselves off from all outside sources that could reason, and thus, shutting themselves off to reason. This is someone who is willing to express deference in most matters.

Full of Mercy: willing to forgive those around you. Willing to show kindness, despite them not deserving it.

Impartial: The subject for our impartiality is vague. It could mean impartial to others, which is a callback to chapter 2! We are looking to others and are willing to know, meet, care for anyone.

It could also mean we are impartial *towards the wisdom.*Some translations render this as “Without doubting” and “without wavering” which would point to this interpretation.

Sincere: Without hypocrisy. Finally this wisdom gives us these qualities, sincerely. We are not hypocritical and speaking about these qualities, but not living it out. Instead we see these qualities in our heart, in our speech, with others.

To close this section, James pulls some qualities that are shown from verse 17.

> [!question] ❓
> **Can you see which qualities in 17 are referenced in 18?**
>
> - Harvest = fruit v. 17
> - Peace = peaceable v. 17

James plucks these ideas of peace and good fruit and spins them into the application.

**Those who sow peace, harvest righteousness.**

If you look to make peace with others (meaning you have received true wisdom from above), you be able to collect righteousness. Right-living!

This reminder wraps the section so well. If you want to be wise, be righteous and meek. If you have found yourself wrapped in wisdom from below. Repent. Instead set about the task of seeking wisdom from above so that you may scatter the seeds of peace in your life. You reward for doing so will _not_ be disorder and evil. Instead your reward will be righteousness.
