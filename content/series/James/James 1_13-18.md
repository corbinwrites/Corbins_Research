## Our sinful hearts (v.13-15)

### Temptation does NOT come from God

James issues another command. We are _not_ to say that we are being tempted by God. He gives us to reasons as to why God is **not** the source of temptation.

> [!question] ❓
> Anybody want to try summarizing the reasons in verse 13 that God is not the source of temptation?

**God’s Character**_First_ with an appeal to** God’s character**. He points out that God _cannot be_ tempted with evil. God is far above evil! He’s the creator and author of life. If God was able to be tempted by evil, that would put evil in a position that commanded God, thus making evil stronger than God. Which would mean evil, as a trait, would *be *God. That is not the case though. Simply, God cannot be tempted with evil**Realization of God’s character**

_Second_ James talks about the realization of God’s character, he will not tempt anyone. Because he can’t be tempted with evil, he won’t tempt someone else with evil. This is a logical step. If I came into small group and I was like, really about Crossfit. Go to my crossfit gym everyday. Doing crazy fast pull-ups that could wrench my shoulders from my sockets. You know, it just was _me_. We would say that’s part of my character. So then if you had a conversation with me, again, have my crossfit shirt on, protein bottle in one hand. What do you think I’m going to talk about? Crossfit. It’s in my character and it’s _realization_ of my character.

In a similar way to how a crossfitter would talk crossfit, so would one who is evil exercise evil. We see that is very clearly not the case with God though. Look back in verse 8 God is described as one who give generously and without reproach. If he was to tempt, he would be neither of these things. The two natures could not exist within God.

So we can very clearly see that the source of temptation is not God from these two reasons. So What is the source of temptation?

### Desires Controlled by Us (v. 14-15)

James immediately answers our question, “each person is tempted… by his own desire.” Look at that phrase. Double ownership. Each person, “his own.” Isn’t that fascinating?

But temptation doesn’t come from nothing, instead there is something hunting, trying to trap us. Notice that word “lure” and “enticed”, the LSB/NASB reads “carried away.” These are hunting words. It’s a fisherman with a hook and a hunter laying a trap for an animal.

What is the name of our hunter?
*Our own desire.*Yes, our individual desire.

> [!question] ❓
> In verses 14-15, James writes that people are lured by their desires and that desire gives birth to sin. Does this make all desires wrong or bad? Support your answer from the passage.
> No. It’s important to note that things change when _desire hunts us_. Ultimately it is when, \*\*we are no longer in charge of the desire but the desire is in charge of us.
>
> \*\*Note that the desire here can give birth to sin. If the desire is incapable of giving birth to sin, it will not. For example, say you desire a fruit of the spirit, such as patience. If you desire patience, and seek to grow it in a Godly way, this desire will not result in sin and death.
>
> Additionally, note that _every good and perfect gift_ is from above. This can include good godly desires!

When is desire not ok? When we are “lured” and “enticed” by the desire. When we are things start to go downhill _fast_.

> [!question] ❓
> **James likens this change to pregnancy. Why do you think he describes it that way?**
> It can take a long time for our desire to grow to sin. It can be a long time for us to see it become full sin. It’s painful. You must bear it like a heavy load. After all this we find that it is not a beautiful child but an ugly monster, sin.

Sin is the very thing that prevents us from entering Heaven. It is the metaphorical nail that pinned our Lord Jesus to the cross and pierced his side. It manifests itself in many ways but in every way it seeks to usurp control from the King in heaven. It is disobedience to God and his Word. There is nothing beautiful about this corruption that our desire gives birth to.

To make matters worse as this corruption grows and grows it begins to tear at your life, pulling you further and further from God, until one day it kills you. This death is worse than a physical one, it is a spiritual one. By letting our desire fester and grow into sin, we have placed our hope in something temporary. Something that withers like the grass and on the day when the sun rises. We will sadly find that we have perished along with the flower. The reason? Because we have given our life to our desire and not to God.

So we leave verses 13-15, with an explicit command, “Don’t say God is tempting you.” and an implicit command, “Control your desire, don’t let it control you.” James moves to his next command for us. He tells us to not be deceived! Ok! We better listen up, what could we be deceived about?

## God’s Good and Perfect Gifts (v.16-18)

> [!question] ❓
> Did anyone find a connection from v.13-15 and v.16-18?
> My own thinking here, I saw it as Our Desires and Desires Controlled by God.

### Desires controlled by God (Corbin’s Connection)

Here James has been talking about about desires and how they can be bring forth sin, however, he commands us to not be fooled! Don’t be deceived! There *are *good gifts that still come from above.

In what I am dubbing a signature James move, James pivots and expands. In my opinion, he performs a similar maneuver in the transition from verses 2-4 to 5-8 where he moves to talk about prayer in a broad sense. The closest literary device I can find to this is amplification, but it doesn’t fully land.

Encompassed within the broad language of a good gift and a perfect gift, it would be reasonable to include a desire that brings glory to God.

> [!question] ❓
> What are some good godly **desires** that come from above?
> A desire to spend time in God’s Word, a desire to talk about matters of the faith with a brother or sister, a desire to serve just to help

Note that this desire, **must** come from above, our natural state is sin and evil. Left to our own devices we’ll craft desires that result in sin. Remember where Jeremiah says,

> The heart is deceitful above all things,

    and desperately sick;
    who can understand it?

### Everything good

However, I don’t want to lose the forest for the trees here. Let’s zoom out an acknowledge what James is saying plainly here, <u>every</u> good and perfect gift comes from the Lord. If you have a good thing happen, praise God. He has given it to you.

Note the descriptor “Father of Lights.” A more specific translation would be Father of the Sun, The Moon, and The Stars.

- Douglas Moo makes the point that James may be selecting this quality of God to highlight the beauty and joy of creation! If you’ve had a beautiful day or night, praise God, what a good gift.
- John MacArthur and Warren Wiersbe makes the point that James may have done this as a wordplay with the shifting shadows. While the celestial bodies move and change. They are wax and wane, they are eclipsed. He does not. He is unchanging. He is the same today, yesterday, and tomorrow. He will _consistently_ give every good and perfect gift

### Salvation

However the greatest gift that God gives, James holds for verse 18.

> [!question] ❓
> In verse 18, James writes that, “Of his (God’s) own will he brought us forth…” What did God bring us forth _into_. To usher forth means that you must be in one spot and go to another spot. What spot have we been brought into?
> _Salvation_

Yes, the best gift is salvation. The best gift he gave us was not even a good desire within ourselves. The best gift that he gave us was his desire for us! Note how James says it is, “of his own will.” God desires you and me and everyone in this room. He has desired you.

> [!question] ❓
> He has brought us forth into salvation, but how?
> _His Word of Truth, the Bible. He makes us a new creation thanks to his word (OT and NT!)_

> [!question] ❓
> What is the result of being saved by the bible?
> We are a firstfruit.
>
> J Mac, “We are the first and best harvest. Christians are the first evidence of God’s new creation that is to come (2 Peter 3:10-13) and enjoy presently in their new life a foretaste of future glory.”

## Outro

As James concludes this section on temptation with salvation, let us take his encouragement and reminder of the best gift we have received as an exhortation as well. Wiersbe uses the analogy of our heart being an apartment shared by two people, the old Adam and the new Adam (Jesus). When temptation knocks on our heart, who will you let answer the door? Let us not forget that we are firstfruits, living proof of the new creation that is awaiting us at Jesus’ second coming. When desires sour and temptation manifests, let us respond as Christians and not give in.
