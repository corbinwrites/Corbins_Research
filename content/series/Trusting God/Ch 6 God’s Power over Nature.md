## Group Devotional

In this chapter, Bridges emphasized how God can, and will, afflict us to simply to show his work. He helpfully provided [[Bible/ESV/John/John 9|John 9]] to prove this. I wanted to spend a bit more time in [[Bible/ESV/John/John 9|John 9]] just to emphasize how much God’s work shines in the blind man’s life.

The blind man’s life shines in a dark world. I was thinking of an example and thought of when I have been intently focused on a conversation or playing a game with someone that I hadn’t realized that it has grown dark? So someone will flip on the light or the streetlights will come on you realize how dark it had been. The work of God is similar. When God works in us it illuminates out from us, like a streetlight or “the big light.” The source of this light is God and it cuts through the darkness.

3. **The Blind Man can physically see**
   1. Note: he does not yet spiritually see. We see that in [[Bible/ESV/John/John 9#17|John 9:17]] (”prophet”) and 9:25 (”I don’t know if he is a sinner”)
   2. However, even without fully believing God’s work will already be on display. It will stand in contrast to:
      1. **Blind ignorance of his Neighbors (v. 8-12)**
         1. The work God has done stands in contrast to the ignorance to the neighbors.
         2. They had spent their entire life passing this man by but when given the chance some **do not** believe the formerly blind man. The reality of the work is too great for them to consider that a miracle had been worked. Yet in stark contrast to their ignorance is the very blind man they passed, standing before them seeing with new eyes. God’s work is displayed here to the neighbors.
      2. **Blind legalism of the Pharisees (v. 13-17)** 3. The work God has done stands in contrast to the legalism of the Pharisees. 4. Here the Pharisees claim that no man of God would do work on the Sabbath. Yet here Jesus very evidently has done the work of God on the Sabbath. God’s work _can_ be done on the Sabbath.
      3. **Blind fear of his Parents (v. 18-25)** 5. The work God has done stands in contrast to the fear of the Parents. 6. Here the parents do not rightly attribute the work to God, through his son, Jesus who is Christ. They are fearful of being put out of the synagogue. However, this work that is displayed before them is proof that Jesus IS the Christ. Though they are fearful, they could have safely made this claim. 7. Thankfully the formerly blind man is bold enough to give the truth. (In verse 24 we learn these are the same Jews who he previously referred to Jesus as a prophet to)
      4. **Blind disbelief of the Jews (v. 26-34)** 8. The work God has done stands in contrast to the disbelief of the Jews 9. The Jews refuse to acknowledge the divinity of Jesus, claiming they don’t know where he comes from 1. The formerly blind man, is shocked! As someone who has only be able to see for 24 hours, even he can see that Jesus is sent from God. (v.30) 2. He astutely points out in v.33 that Jesus must come from God to have worked his power. 10. The Jews cast the formerly blind man out of the temple, despite their best attempts to not believe, it’s so clear that this work is *from *God. Yet they choose not to believe.
      5. **What do we learn about the work of God that is displayed**
      - It can contrast our human daily thinking, our usual thoughts
        - “A blind beggar can’t see”
        - “This has come around because of the consequences of an action”
      - It contrasts our legalistic thinking
        - “God would never work on the Sabbath”
        - “God doesn’t work that way.” He gets to make the rules
      - It should always be acknowledged, even if we are fearful
        - “Admitting the work of God to others, might mean they persecute me.”
      - Despite what man may believe, it is clearly from God.
4. **True sight** 3. The best for last, the man does have true sight at the end. He sees that this “prophet” was really the Christ and he believes in him. 4. Isn’t this the true end of God’s work? For God to receive Glory: 6. First, the form of worship from a believer. 7. Second, the judgement of those who claim to incorrectly, they are actually blind.

5. The Blind Man can See
6. Blind ignorance of his Neighbors
7. Blind legalism of the Pharisees
8. Blind fear of his Parents
9. Blind disbelief of the Jews
10. True sight

## **Developing Trust**

11. Summarize in your own words what each of these verses says about God’s role in natural phenomena.
    - Job 37:3, 6, 10-13: These verses describe how God controls and directs natural phenomena like thunder, rain, snow, and clouds.
    - [[Bible/ESV/Psalms/Ps 147#8|Psalm 147:8]], 16-18: This passage highlights how God provides for animals by giving them food, controlling the weather, and guiding migration patterns.
    - Jeremiah 10:13; 14:22: These verses emphasize that God is the one who brings rain and controls the seasons.
    - Amos 4:7-9: In this passage, God demonstrates His power by causing droughts, withholding rain, and sending destructive weather as a means of discipline.
12. a. What is your usual attitude toward the weather? Do you tend to complain about it? Thank God for it? Feel that God isn’t responsible?
    - b. Is your attitude generally pleasing to God? Why or why not?
13. a. What is God taking responsibility for in [[Bible/ESV/Exodus/Exod 4#11|Exodus 4:11]] and [[Bible/ESV/Isaiah/Isa 45#7|Isaiah 45:7]]?

    - c. Which response do you choose? Why?

## **Men’s/Women’s Developing Trust**

15. The idea that God claims responsibility for deadly earthquakes, droughts, blindness, and childlessness prompts sensitive people to ask questions. Certainly we can’t learn much without asking questions.
    - a. Are there limits to the kinds of questions we should ask of God? If so, what limits? If not, why not?
    - b. Can we expect God to answer our questions? Why or why not?
    - c. What kinds of attitudes are acceptable when questioning God?
    - d. What kinds of attitudes are unacceptable?
    - e. Give some possible reasons why God leaves many of our questions unanswered.
16. a. Describe an experience in your life that caused you to doubt God’s control and to ask why.
    - b. What answers did you get to your questioning?
    - c. Did anything good come from that experience? If so, what was it?
17. How can coming to grips with the fact that God is in control of nature affect your daily life, especially your current circumstances?
