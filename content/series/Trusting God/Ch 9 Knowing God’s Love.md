# Group Devotional

> “When we are in the midst of adversity and, as it frequently seems to happen, calamity after calamity seems to be surging in upon us, we will be tempted to doubt God’s love.”
> Excerpt From
> Trusting God
> Jerry Bridges

So is the case with Jeremiah!

Outline:

Jeremiah Feels (v.16 -21)

Jeremiah Knows (v. 22-24)

Result (v. 31-33)

### Jeremiah Feels (v.16 -20)

- Jeremiah says that God has made his teeth grind on gravel and cower in ashes

> [!question] ❓
> How does that feel? What emotion is in that?

- Due to the affliction Jeremiah says that he has no peace. That he has forgotten happiness
- In desperation, Jeremiah links his endurance and hope. Since I can go no further, so my hope in the Lord is gone
  - Spiritual burnout
- In this desperate, depressed state he asks the Lord to remember his affliction.

> [!question] ❓
> What is meant by wormwood and gall? How does this describe the affliction and wanderings?
>
> - It’s really bitter and difficult
>   - Wormwood can be fermented to vermouth or bitters. It’s a bitter herb.
>   - Gall, also known as bile. The bitter taste in your mouth after you vomit.

- He says, Lord I remember it and it makes me sad. My soul is bowed in sadness.
- Note: He’s reminding the Lord, God who knows all things and orchestrates all things of this sadness.
  - Haven’t we had points in our life where we have done this?
    - Personally, I’ve been really struggling with work. The people I support want a perfect delivery. No bugs, build to spec (even if they change specifications during the process), and on time. I see that it is futile. There is no perfection on this world. Something will always go wrong.
    - So I look to God and I say, “God, can’t remember how hard this is at work?”

### Jeremiah Knows (v. 21-24)

- However, Jeremiah instead recalls something that brings him hope. He “calls to mind.” Rather than sit in his feelings, he puts what he knows to work. He combats his downcast soul with the truth of God’s character. He recalls: God’s Steadfast Love and Ceaseless Mercy.
  - ## Steadfast Love
  - Ceaseless Mercy
- These two characteristics are renewed everyday. _You can count on them_. Just as the sun _will always_ break through the storm clouds, so will love and mercy break through each and everyday. _But you must call it to mind, just as Jeremiah did. _
- These two characteristics drive Jeremiah to exclaim in praise. “Great is your faithfulness!” - Despite his sadness, his bitterness, he _must_ exclaim about how incredible it is that God is faithful - God is faithful despite Jeremiah’s _own_ faithlessness. Remember in ver 18, where he says “his hope has perished?” Despite that statement the Lord shows himself _to be faithful_. Jeremiah, rather than sitting in despair, focuses on the fact that God will be faithful.
  > [!question] ❓
  > What does it mean that God will be faithful?
  > \*That God will follow his promise.

Look at verses 31-36. This is a depiction of faithfulness. God will not cast off forever. God does not approve of incorrect justice. It will be corrected. This is faithfulness.\*

- The Lord is my “portion” - can either refer to your “destiny or your lot”, but also a legal term referring to a part of an estate. I think the legal term fits well here. Yahweh is our inheritance (Ephesians 1:11). It is a guarantee for those who believe.
  - Since we know we have a guarantee that we have God. We have his steadfast love and ceaseless mercy. Jeremiah reminds himself, perhaps persuades himself, that he will _hope_ in God. Despite the bitter hardship, there is a guarantee of love and mercy.
