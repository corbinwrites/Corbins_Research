# Group Devotional #1

1 Kings 16-19

Context: Ahab is the latest wicked king in Israel (coming into power at 874 BC). He marries a Sidonian princess named Jezebel, who is all about Baal. Ahab is such a proponent for this idol that he builds a temple and sets gates up in Jericho at the cost of two of his sons in 1 Kings 16:34 (as foretold by Joshua). His wife is done with Yahweh and sends out to have the prophets destroyed. Ahab and Jezebel are bad and strong. They are formidable government officials.

Underneath Ahab is Obadiah, we would call him his Chief of Staff or Chief of Operations. He loves God. He hides some of the prophets when they are persecuted (1 Kings 18:3-4). God sends Elijah to talk with Obadiah. **Read 1 Kings 18:7-16**

We see that Obadiah is filled with anxiety, with doubt about what the Lord commands.

(Remember Philippians 4:6 commands us to “not be anxious about anything, but in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known to the Lord.”)

He’s so anxious he says twice, “now you say go tell your lord” (v. 11, 14) you can just picture the fretting in Obadiah. Here he must go against the government. The head of the nation of Israel at this point. He fears for his life!

> [!question] ❓
> How does this resolve?

Obadiah meets with Ahab then Ahab meets with Elijah.

There is no fanfare, no narrow avoidance of death. The Lord is sovereign over all things. Obadiah was safe from the moment that Elijah relayed the Lord’s request. We see the Lord had placed some amount of understanding, perhaps even compassion, in the heart of Ahab to preserve Obadiah. God is at work in the highest places of government, even with desperately wicked leaders..

And then Daniel gives him a ray of hope in the vision. **Verse 26**, ‘In that day it was commanded to leave the stump with the roots of the tree, your kingdom will be assured to you after you recognize that it is Heaven that rules.’” That’s the only time in the Old Testament that heaven is used in place of the term “God,” that refers to God. You’ll get your kingdom back when you acknowledge God. Until then, you will be a madman, a maniac, a beast. “‘It was commanded to leave the stump with the roots of the tree because your kingdom will be assured to you, but not until you recognize that it is heaven that rules.’”

You know, that’s what the world has to recognize. The leaders of the world have to recognize that God rules and submit themselves to Him and obey Him and do His will as revealed in Holy Scripture. And that’s what Daniel says to his king. “‘Therefore, O king, may my advice be pleasing to you: break away now from your sins by doing righteousness and from your iniquities by showing mercy to the poor, in case there may be a prolonging of your prosperity.’” That’s an invitation. And you know what he’s saying? If you will repent and if you will go in the path of righteousness and acknowledge the true God, and it will be so genuine it will show up in a transformed life, which will be merciful rather than brutal as you have been. If you will turn from your sin, God will withhold the judgment. God will withhold the judgment.

This is a warning with a promise of blessing for repentance. But the king must break with sin and enter a righteous relationship with God. That’s what we ought to be praying. You know it says in [[Bible/ESV/1 Timothy/1 Tim 2|1 Timothy 2]], “Pray for those that are over you. Pray for the rulers and the kings.” What are you praying? You should be praying for their salvation. You should be praying that they would hear the warnings of Scripture. You should be praying that they would turn from their sin and their wickedness and their unrighteousness to the living and true God. That’s what that text in [[Bible/ESV/1 Timothy/1 Tim 2|1 Timothy 2]] is saying. “For God our Savior is the mediator, the one mediator between God and man, the man Christ Jesus.” Pray for their salvation.

## Group Devotional #3

Context: As we enter Daniel 9, it’s interesting to look back to Daniel 8. We learn in Daniel 8 that Daniel has seen a vision of how Midia and Persia are broken by Greece. God perfectly predicts the rise of Alexander the Great and how he will conquer much of the Mediterranean. God reveals this vision to Daniel and Daniel clearly sees how God knows and controls all nations.

Enter Daniel 9! We see Daniel has found out that Israel’s exile is almost up. They are nearing 70 years in Babylon and he discovers this fact while reading Jeremiah.

> [!question] ❓
> So what does Daniel do when he finds this fact?
> _He prays_
> How does he pray?
> _Confession (v.2-15) and Supplication (v.16-19)_

Daniel largely prays about Israel’s wickedness. How the nation was unfaithful to God. In the theme of how God rules over the nations, I want to pull out a particular verse here: verse 12.

> He has confirmed his words, which he spoke against us and against our rulers who ruled us by bringing upon us a great calamity.

Daniel, rightly, points out that God had made commands not only of the *people *of Israel, but of the _rulers_ of Israel. If it hadn’t been clear already, this verse firmly establishes that it is the _The Lord_ who commands the world. He is the one who is king over all kings, who commands commanders. Note that **we see that God follows through.** Some might make empty commands, but that is not the Lord. What he says will come to pass.
