## Group Homework Devotional

Read, Job 6:14-27

Context: As Jerry Bridges says in the question, “Job lost his children, his wealth, and his health. His friends preached to him about God’s sovereignty.” More specifically, in the previous chapter, Eliphaz, one of Job’s friends, says that the innocent prosper and Job just needs to take the fate that’s been handed to him because he’s clearly not innocent. Job understandably disagrees. He begins chapter 6 with lament and transitions into a response to Eliphaz.

To open let’s tackle question “a”

> [!question] ❓
> **What did Job’s friends do wrong? How did they “bruise” him?**

Job’s critique in his friends flows into two major points their treachery (betraying) and the futility of their reproof.

The word here for a “torrent-bed” is literally a wadi, a valley, ravine or channel that is dry except in the rainy season.

They are vibrant and full of life when there is rain, but when the dry season comes they are gone. Which is exactly what Job writes in verse 17.

![[Assets/Images/Untitled 2.png]]

![[Assets/Images/Untitled 3.png]]

In fact those looking for relief and aid from a wadi are fooled and killed for this! They are thirsty, that need relief from the dry hot sun. They have seen this beautiful oasis before, but it turns out it is a wadi! It’s dry. There is no life in the river at all.

Similarly, Job’s friends are like this! When he has abundance and all these good things in his life, his friends similarly are a source of life and refreshment. One commentator went so far as to say they give good counsel in this time. However, when things get hard and his abundance dries up, his friends have no life to give him. They are not an oasis, that is perpetually life giving, but are instead wither up their love and counsel when his life is hard. Verse 21 confirms this, “You have become nothing; you see my calamity and are afraid.” Job points out that the wadi of friendship melts from **fear**. I would argue this fear is founded in a fear to *engage in the sorrow, *in the hard time of a friend. Instead it is easier to give arms length counsel, that doesn’t seek to understand or empathize but really to correct _what they think_ is present

What is interesting in verse 20, we see the **travelers** are ashamed. This implies they should have known better. Job places himself in the shoes of the traveler and regrets having set foot in this wadi.

Job asks a series of rhetorical questions demonstrate he is not asking for high effort things or sinful things. In fact, if Job had asked for these things, reproof might be necessary.

Job next moves to the futility of his friends’ reproof. He is genuinely asking to be taught something but they have nothing to teach him. It is important to remember here that Job is blameless! He has done nothing wrong. So he asks his friends to teach him, show him the way he has erred and he will be silent and accept it. However, his friends cannot procure an explanation.

Job requires upright words!” - Righteous words are powerful and painful

“what does reproof from your reprove?” - What do you intend to reprove?

“You think you can reprove words, when the speech of despairing man is wind?” - Why are you reproving me when you think my words mean nothing. (8:2 verifies) Like the wind. It’s something that is already gone.

“you would cast lots over orphans and bargain over your friend.” - They are cold and heartless. They see Job only as an object. Something to be dealt with not loved and cared for. We see this in their baseless claims and heartless interactions.

## Group Homework Questions

1. We need to be very sensitive in teaching others about the sovereignty of God and encouraging them to trust God when they are in the midst of problems or pain. It is much easier to trust in the sovereignty of God when it is the other person who is hurting. We need to be like Jesus, of whom it was said, “A bruised reed he will not break” (Matthew 12:20). Job lost his children, his wealth, and his health. His friends preached to him about God’s sovereignty. Read Job’s response to his friends in Job 6:14-27.
   1. What did Job’s friends do wrong? How did they “bruise” him?
   2. If you had a friend in Job’s position, how would you deal with him when he questioned God’s sovereignty and goodness?
