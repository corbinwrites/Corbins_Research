# **Bibliology**

What does the Bible say about itself?

1. God is perfect (1 John 1:5, 3:5)
2. God communicates by verbalizing His thoughts (2 Timothy 3:16, [[Bible/ESV/2 Peter/2 Pet 1#21|2 Peter 1:21]])
3. God intends for the Bible to be understood (Psalm 19:7, 119:130; [[Bible/ESV/Deuteronomy/Deut 30#11|Deuteronomy 30:11-14]])

# **Hermeneutics**

If the Bible is God’s communication to us, we make some conclusions about how tointerpret it. We can understand it:

4. Grammatical
5. Historical
6. Literal
7. Single-meaning

_Hermeneutics_ is a set of principles.

- _Exegesis_ is an implementation of valid interpretive principles.
- _Meaning_ is the truth intention of the author.
- _Interpretation_ is an understanding of the truth intention of the author.

(*Evangelical Hermeneutics,*p. 27)

## **The Sufficiency of Scripture****Definition”** Ligonier: The Bible contains all that we need for determining what we must believe and how we are to live before God. We need no other source of special revelation for**faith and life** in addition to the Bible.

John Piper: The Scriptures are the only inspired and (therefore) inerrant words of God that we need, in order **to know the way of salvation and the way of obedience**. The sufficiency of Scripture means that we don’t need any more special revelation. We don’t need any more inspired, inerrant words. In the Bible God has given us, we have the perfect standard for judging all other knowledge.

# **The Exegetical Process****Observation**:** What does it say?**In this step, we read the text for the sake of gathering details. We are not yet making any conclusions about or based on those details.** Interpretation**:** What does it mean?** In this step, we use the features of the text to come to a conclusion about the author’s intended meaning.

- “Meaning is one, application is many”: There is only one meaning (per the “single meaning” part of our hermeneutics), so it can’t mean different things to different people.
  - Interpretation is not about what it means _to you_, or about what to do. (We’ll get to that in the application step!)
- The meaning is confined to what the historical audience understood (per the “historical” part of our hermeneutics).

**Application**:** Why does it matter?**In this step, we point God’s Word inward to help us live holier, more God-honoring lives (2 Timothy 3:16-17, [[Bible/ESV/1 Thessalonians/1 Thess 4#1|1 Thessalonians 4:1]]-3a).

- Application can also be called “significance” or “implication.”

## **Interpretation**

> [!tip] 💡
> **Key questions**
> Why does the author use these features?
>
> What is the author trying to tell the original audience?
>
> Ultimately, what does it mean?

### Steps

10. Describe the importance of the individual features.
11. Summarize the meaning of the passage into 1-2 sentences. A helpful practice is to start our interpretation with “[The author] says…” or “[The author] tells [the original audience] that…”

## **Application**

> [!tip] 💡
> **Key Questions**
> Is there a theological principle?
>
> How does the passage change the way I think or live?
>
> Ultimately, why does it matter?

### Steps

12. Identify a theological principle. (This may not be distinct from the Interpretation. For example, in [[Bible/ESV/1 Corinthians/1 Cor 15#3|1 Corinthians 15:3-8]], the interpretation is, “Paul says that Jesus died and was raised from the dead.” The theological principle is that Jesus rose from the dead.)
13. Make a conclusion about how the passage affects your life today.
14. Apply the passage!

“_Think over_ what I say, for the Lord will give you understanding in everything” (2 Timothy 2:7). Every book worth reading beckons with the words, “_Think over_ what I say.”… When my sons complain that a book is too hard to read, I say, “Raking is easy, but all you get is leaves; digging is hard, but you might find diamonds.”

- John Piper

### Some Helpful Questions that Can Help Find Application

15. _Are there examples to follow?_
16. _Are there commands to obey?_
17. _Are there errors to avoid?_
18. _Are there sins to forsake?_
19. _Are there promises to claim?_
20. _Are there new thoughts about God?_
21. _Are there principles to live by?_

### How to apply the passage

- Ask personal questions
  - _Commands to obey: conduct yourself worthy of the Gospel, stand firm in unity, strive together for the Gospel (v. 27)_
  - _Errors to avoid: being afraid of opponents of the Gospel (v. 28)_
  - _Promises to claim: opponents of the Gospel will be destroyed (v. 28), those who stand firm will be saved (v. 28)_
  - _Principles to live by: face persecution knowing it is an act of grace from God (v. 29)_
- Meditate on the passage
  - _Now that you’ve already meditated on the question “What does this mean?” in step two, now meditate on the question “What does this mean \***\*for me\*\***?”_
  - _Pray over what changes should be made in your life._
- Develop goals for applying this text. Effective goals are:
  - _Specific: avoid application that is too general to put into practice._
  - _Measurable: avoid application if you cannot track its progress._
  - _Achievable: Avoid application that is not practically feasible._
