# Form
## Teachings of Jesus
1. ASK: Who was Jesus’ audience for this teaching? Identifying the immediate audience of Jesus will help with your interpretation.
2. Most of Jesus’ teaching can be observed, interpreted, and applied similarly to the way we approach the Epistles, so “think paragraphs”! Summarize each paragraph of His long teachings and see how they work together to communicate a larger message.
3. Jesus also gave some imperatives in his teaching - commands to follow - and they often seem somewhat impossible. 

“...to see [Jesus’]  imperatives as law is to misunderstand them. They are not law in the sense that one must obey them in order to become or remain a Christian; our salvation does not depend on perfect obedience to them. Rather, they are descriptions, by way of imperative, of what Christian life should be like because of God’s prior acceptance of us.” ~ How to Read the Bible for All Its Worth
## Narrative
1. The narrative portions of the Gospels typically fall into two main categories.
	1. Stories about Jesus to illustrate something about Him or about our broken human condition.
	2. Stories within His teaching to illustrate what is being taught.

# Keys to Interpretation
## Ask two main interpretive questions:
1. What is taught in this episode (by Jesus or about Jesus)?
2. What is taught by the way the episodes are linked together to form the larger story?
##  Read the individual stories/episodes.
1. Ask and answer the standard narrative questions. 
   (Who? What? When? Where? Why? How?)
2. Look for interpretive instructions from the author. 
3. The Gospel author will often help readers see his point by offering clues in the introduction or conclusion. The last line of an episode can hold the primary message of the episode. (ex. Luke 14:7, Mark 4:41, Mark 7:19)
4. Pay special attention to any repeated element.
	1. Words (ex. John 15 – “abide,” Matthew 23 – “woe”)
	2. Themes (ex. Luke 12:22-34 – repeated “do not worry”)
	3. Characters made prominent
5. Watch for places where the story shifts to direct discourse.
	1. Isolate the direct discourse to see the heart of the story. This often provides a very clear perspective to the theological message of the story.
## Read a series of stories.
1. Look for connections between the individual episodes.
	1. Common themes
	2. Patterns
	3. Logical connections (ex. cause and effect)
	4. How the episodes are joined (transitions or conjunctions)
	5. How the stories differ
	6. Compare characters
	7. Pay close attention to Jesus - His identity, mission, teaching, and how people respond to Him
## Be aware of parallel stories in the other Gospels. 
1. Exercise caution in this - the authors’ intents were not to be read in tandem and compared to one another. However, God has provided the four gospels in the canon of scripture, and so they cannot be read in isolation from one another either.
2. Don’t “fill out” the stories. Read the other versions of a story to appreciate the distinctives of each of the gospels, and the contexts of the different audiences.
